ASSESSMENT OF SUBLINGUAL MICROCIRCULATION TO EVALUATE THE EFFICACY OF 
RESUSCITATION THERAPY IN SEPTIC SHOCK PATIENTS: A COHORT STUDY
Xiaolei Zhang,1,2 Haisong Zhang,1,2 Rui Jin,1,2 Li Li,1,2 Li Huang,1,2 Zhanwen Wang,1,2 Qianyi Peng,1,2 
Meilin Ai,1,2 and Lina Zhang1,2
1Department of Critical Care Medicine, Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, Changsha, China; 2National Clinical Research 
Center for Geriatric Disorders, Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, Changsha, China
Received 27 Mar 2025; first review completed 13 Apr 2025; accepted in final form 13 Aug 2025
ABSTRACT—Objectives: Determining the endpoint of resuscitation in septic shock is essential to enhance effective -
ness, prevent over-resuscitation, and improve outcomes. We designed this study to investigate whether sublingual 
microcirculation monitoring can serve as an effective marker for assessing the efficacy of resuscitation therapy in septic 
shock. Methods: A total of 72 septic shock patients were included in our final analysis, excluding those with heart 
function impairments, including sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy. Sublingual microcirculation parameters were mea -
sured at two time points: before resuscitation and 6 hours postresuscitation. Additionally, the values of macrocircula -
tory parameters, blood gas analysis variables, and organ prognosis indicators were collected at multiple time points 
before and after resuscitation. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to assess the correlations among these 
variables. Furthermore, the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis method was employed to evaluate the pre -
dictive performance of sublingual microcirculation parameters and other relevant factors for patient prognosis. Finally, 
we determined the optimal threshold of proportion of perfused vessel (PPV)6h by anchoring it to three key aspects: tissue 
oxygenation (Lac 24h), organ dysfunction progression ( △APACHE II3d and △SOFA3d), and long-term outcomes (adverse 
prognosis and 28-day mortality). Results: Sublingual microcirculation variables postresuscitation showed no significant 
correlation with conventional circulation variables. PPV6h had the highest predictive efficacy for 28-day prognosis, better 
than PcvO26h, the best predictor among conventional variables. The optimal PPV 6h threshold (68.6%) was determined 
using three key criteria mentioned above. Patients meeting this threshold after resuscitation showed improved microcir-
culation (lower lactate levels and faster clearance), reduced organ dysfunction (lower APACHE II and SOFA scores, less 
need for continuous renal replacement therapy), and better long-term outcomes (fewer vasoactive drugs, 28-day lower 
mortality). Conclusions: Our study highlights the potential utility of sublingual microcirculation as an adjunctive tool for 
reflecting the effectiveness of resuscitation therapy and proposes that the PPV 6h >68.6% may serve as a target for early 
goal-directed therapy in future studies.

icine, with complex pathophysiology, which encompasses 
cytokine storms, endothelial dysfunction, microcirculatory 
disturbances, and uncontrolled immune cascades ( 2). Early 
and adequate fluid resuscitation is widely recognized as a 
cornerstone of septic shock management. Numerous clin -

The current clinical implementation of septic shock bun -
dle therapy predominantly adheres to the principle-oriented 
recommendations detailed in continuously updated guide -
lines. Timely determination of the resuscitation endpoint 
requires a careful balance between restoring microcircula -
tory perfusion, normalizing tissue oxygen metabolism, and 
preventing fluid overload. The criteria for terminating ini -
tial resuscitation therapy remain relatively ambiguous ( 7,8). 
Macrocirculation indicators such as heart rate, blood pres -
sure, and central venous pressure (CVP) are easily obtain -
able, but can be influenced by various factors and may not 
reflect microcirculatory perfusion or cellular metabolism 
accurately ( 9–12). Therefore, their normalization does 
not guarantee the resolution of shock’s pathophysiology 
(4,6). Among the clinical blood gas analysis indicators, 
central venous oxygen saturation (ScvO2) and central 
venous-to-arterial carbon dioxide difference (Pcv-aCO2) 
indirectly reflect systemic microcirculatory perfusion 
and the balance between oxygen supply and consumption 
(13,14). Hematocrit (Hct) indicates the circulatory system’s 
oxygen delivery capacity and hemodilution due to fluid 
infusion, which also influences microcirculation. Changes 
in these three variables are influenced by factors such as 
tissue heterogeneity of oxygen metabolism, altered oxygen 
uptake capacity, and cardiac function ( 15). Blood lactate 
level (Lac) is a marker that reflect the severity of multiple 
pathological changes in patients with septic shock, includ -
ing enhanced anaerobic metabolism in tissues, microcircu -
latory dysfunction, and impaired oxygen utilization, etc. 
However, due to its relatively slow decline in circulation, 
Lac may exhibit delayed responsiveness and reduced accu -
racy when assessing the adequacy of resuscitation therapy 
(16,17). Compared with conventional indicators, sublin -
gual microcirculation monitoring using side-stream dark 
field (SDF) imaging demonstrates significant potential as 
a more suitable reference metric. This technique provides 
direct visualization of tissue microcirculation and has been 
validated in clinical settings ( 18,19). Previous studies have 
established a robust correlation between sublingual micro -
circulation findings and the prognosis of patients with sep -
tic shock ( 20,21). Owing to its low technical complexity 
and high clinical applicability, this method has been widely 
adopted in intensive care units (ICUs), thereby facilitating 
broader dissemination.
Numerous studies have shown significant differences in 
microcirculatory and macrocirculatory indicators among 
subgroups of septic shock patients based on their progno -
sis (15,22). The current priority is to identify reliable mark -
ers to determine the endpoint of fluid resuscitation, guiding 
ICU physicians to adjust treatment strategies promptly. We 
designed this study to investigate whether sublingual micro -
circulation monitoring can serve as an effective tool for 
assessing the efficacy of resuscitation therapy in patients 
with septic shock.
METHODS
Overall study design
This medical research was approved by the Medical Ethics 
Committee (Approval No. 201903025) and registered on the 
clinical trial website (ClinicalTrials.gov, ID: NCT03859726). 
All included patients voluntarily participated and signed the 
informed consent form, either personally or via authorized rep-
resentatives. Our study included all septic shock patients trans-
ferred to the Central ICU of Xiangya Hospital, Central South 
University, from March 2019 to March 2021. In consideration 
of the reproducibility of research findings and adherence to 
the guideline-recommended bundle for septic shock manage -
ment, we focused our analysis on sublingual microcirculation 
measurements obtained at 6 hours postresuscitation. Initially, 
we confirmed the mismatch between micro- and macrocircu -
lation variables following resuscitation in septic shock patients 
(Fig. 1), thereby highlighting microcirculation’s role in assess-
ing resuscitation efficacy. Subsequently, using 28-day mortality 
as the outcome measure, we evaluated the predictive capacity 
of various Microsee-derived variables (Fig. 2 and Table 1) and 
compared these with microcirculatory indices derived from 
blood gas analysis and macrocirculatory variables obtained 
through monitoring (Supplementary Figures S1, https://links.
lww.com/SHK/C614, and S2, https://links.lww.com/SHK/
C615, and Table 2). This comparison provided preliminary 
evidence supporting the utility of microcirculation in assess -
ing resuscitation effectiveness, identifying the proportion of 
perfused vessels (PPV)-6h as the strongest prognostic predic -
tor. To further refine the efficiency of resuscitation therapy, we 
employed receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analy-
sis to evaluate the predictive performance of PPV-6h regarding 
changes in blood lactate levels, organ dysfunction scores, and 
adverse outcomes (Supplementary Figures S3, https://links.
lww.com/SHK/C616, and S4, https://links.lww.com/SHK/
C617, and Table 3), yielding highly consistent results. Based 
on these analyses, we selected 68.6% as the optimal cutoff 
value for PPV-6h. Finally, we compared differences in post -
resuscitation microcirculatory variables and short-term/long-
term prognostic outcomes between the PPV-6h ≤68.6% group 
and the PPV-6h >68.6% group ( Fig. 3, Tables 4 and 5, and 
Supplementary Table S5, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C618), 
thereby validating the practicality of this cutoff threshold.
Selection criteria
Consecutive adult patients (≥18 years) with septic shock 
admitted to the ICU were considered eligible. Septic shock was 
defined as the presence of infection or suspicion of infection, 
accompanied by a Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) 
score ≥2 that persisted despite adequate fluid resuscitation, hypo-
tension requiring vasopressors to maintain a mean arterial pres-
sure (MAP) ≥65 mmHg, and blood lactate levels >2 mmol/L.
Exclusion criteria
Patients were excluded if they had active bleeding, had 
severe cardiac dysfunction, were pregnant, received a diagnosis 
of septic shock more than four hours before enrollment, or had 
been given a mortality risk within the next 24 hours.

Evaluation process and parameter acquisition for 
sublingual microcirculation metrics
For each septic shock patient included in our study, sublin -
gual microcirculation was assessed twice: before fluid resus -
citation (denoted as Pre) and 6 hours after the diagnosis of 
sepsis (denoted as 6h; immediate emergency treatment for sep-
tic shock was initiated upon diagnosis). All assessments were 
performed using the same device (MicroSee, V100, Guangzhou 
Medsoft System Ltd., Guangdong, China; SDF probe light 
source parameters: 530 nm green lighting), with each evalua -
tion completed within 30 minutes. Upon transferring a septic 
shock patient to the ICU or making a new diagnosis, the front-
line clinical team leaders (Li Li, Li Huang, Zhanwen Wang, 
and Qianyi Peng) promptly contacted Xiaolei Zhang, who 
arrived at the bedside within 30 minutes to conduct the prere -
suscitation assessment, with details regarding the associated 
equipment and procedures provided in Supplementary Figure 
S1, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C614. The second assessment 
was performed by Xiaolei Zhang at the corresponding 6-hour 
time point, with a permissible time deviation of no more than 1 
hour. To ensure methodological rigor, we implemented blinding 
in personnel allocation. Specifically, the four frontline clinical 
team leaders were blinded to the sublingual microcirculation 
results (this examination was not part of routine clinical care 
during the study period, and no associated fees were charged), 
ensuring homogeneity in treatment protocols. Rui Jin, respon -
sible for the initial analyzing of the collected sublingual micro-
circulation images, and Xiaolei Zhang, the assistant recorder, 
were unaware of the patients’ clinical information, remained 
unaware of the patients’ clinical information to maintain consis-
tency in data collection and image analysis. Finally, Meilin Ai, 
the designated technician who had undergone relevant technical 
training and remained blinded to the clinical information, was 
responsible for overseeing the quality control of all examination 
data and images. The single sublingual microcirculation exam-
ination process is as follows: fully expose the sublingual tissue 
while ensuring it is not injured by objects such as endotracheal 
tubes. Gently remove saliva using gauze moistened with normal 
saline, then acquire stable and clear microvascular images for 
at least 20 seconds from three distinct positions (left, middle, 
and right) under the tongue. Ensure that pressure artifacts are 
avoided during image acquisition. Following the examination, 
analyze the acquired images using the sublingual microcircula-
tion imaging system management software (Guangzhou Yiruan 
Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., Guangdong, China). Draw the 
centerlines and lumen boundaries of blood vessels and classify 
them based on lumen diameter. Using the software’s automated 
function, collect data for small blood vessels (diameter < 20 
μm), including total vascular density (TVD), perfused vascular 
density (PVD), perfused vascular proportion (PPV), microvas-
cular flow index (MFI), and heterogeneity index (HI). Calculate 
the mean of the data obtained from the three video segments to 
serve as the final direct result for evaluating the microcirculation 
status of the corresponding patient.

Statistical analysis

RESULTS
General patient characteristics
As depicted in Figure 4 and Supplementary Table S1, 
https://links.lww.com/SHK/C619, during the study period, a 
total of 72 patients were included in the final analysis, predom-
inantly elderly males, with primary sources of infection mainly 
including pneumonia, intra-abdominal infections, and urinary 
tract infections.
Analysis of synchrony in Sublingual Microcirculation and 
Macrocirculation
To evaluate the necessity of sublingual microcirculation 
assessment during fluid resuscitation in septic shock, we ana -
lyzed the correlation between sublingual microcirculation vari-
ables at 6 hours postresuscitation and simultaneously measured 
macrocirculation variables, as presented in Figure 1. Except for 
a weak correlation between MFI and ScvO2 (Spearman R = 
0.295, P = 0.019) as well as Pv-aCO2 (Spearman R = −0.277, 
P = 0.028), no significant correlations were observed in other 
tests. These findings suggest that normalization of macrocircu-
lation does not necessarily equate to restoration of tissue per -
fusion. Furthermore, variables derived solely from blood gas 
analysis (Lac, ScvO2, Pcv-aCO2, Hct) and continuous moni -
toring (HR, MAP) are insufficient for accurately predicting the 
endpoint of resuscitation.
Prediction of 28-day mortality based on sublingual 
microcirculation or macrocirculation variables
Given that numerous prior studies have established the prog-
nostic predictive value of sublingual microcirculation variables 
(20,21,25), we categorized the enrolled patients according to 
their 28-day survival status to further evaluate the potential 
utility of these variables in guiding resuscitation for septic 
shock. No significant differences were observed between the 
two groups regarding demographic characteristics, infection 
source distribution, or ICU length of stay. However, at baseline, 
nonsurvivors exhibited significantly higher HR, lower Hct and 
ScvO2 levels, and more severe organ dysfunction as indicated 
by APACHE II and SOFA scores. At 6 hours postresuscitation, 
macrocirculatory variables in nonsurvivors showed greater 
deterioration, with significantly elevated Lac, Hct, and ScvO2 
levels (Supplementary Tables S1, https://links.lww.com/SHK/
C619, and S2, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C620). In terms of 
direct sublingual microcirculation measurements, no signifi -
cant intergroup differences were noted at baseline. Following 
resuscitation, however, survivors demonstrated significantly 
higher TVD, PVD, PPV , and MFI values, along with a lower 
HI, as shown in Supplementary Tables S3, https://links.lww.
com/SHK/C621.
We conducted ROC curve analysis to assess the predictive 
efficacy of sublingual microcirculation variables measured 
at 6 hours postresuscitation for 28-day outcomes. Positive 
results were observed across all five indicators, as presented in 
Figure 2 and Table 1. Among these, PPV 6h exhibited the most 
robust predictive performance, characterized by the highest 
AUC value and Youden index. PVD6h and MFI 6h also demon -
strated notable predictive efficacy; however, PVD6h is an abso-
lute value indicator, whereas MFI6h is automatically calculated 
by software based on selected images. Given the potential 
influence of variations in sublingual microcirculation detection 
instruments used across different countries and regions on the 
generalizability of our findings, we selected PPV 6h as the pri -
mary indicator for data analysis. In addition, we performed the 
same ROC curve analysis using sublingual microcirculation 
variables measured at baseline; however, no significant positive 
results were observed (TVD, P = 0.4364; PVD, P = 0.1043; 
PPV , P = 0.1410; MFI, P = 0.3550; HI, P = 0.5425).
As shown in Table 2 and the Supplementary Figures S1,

its limited impact on septic shock prognosis in prior studies 
and the presence of numerous confounding factors. Among 
lactate-related indicators, Lac 24h exhibited the highest pre -

index values surpassing those of PPV 6h. Consequently, Lac 24h 
was incorporated as a resuscitation endpoint indicator, with a

Evaluating the diagnostic efficacy of PPV6h >68.6% for 
assessing the effectiveness of resuscitation treatment: 
Organ-specific and overall prognosis
when the observation period was extended to 72 hours post-
resuscitation, the degree of organ dysfunction deterioration 
(ΔAPACHE II72h and ΔSOFA 72h) in the PPV 6h >68.6% group 
was significantly reduced, and the incidence and extent of 
new-onset organ dysfunction were significantly lower (both 
groups predominantly exhibited acute kidney injury; how -
ever, the PPV6h ≤68.6% group also experienced acute hepatic 
dysfunction and delirium), as shown in Figure 2 and Table 5. 
The demand for organ support treatment was also lower in 
the PPV 6h >68.6% group (no significant difference in MV 
usage, but a significantly lower proportion of patients required 
CRRT). Additionally, during this period, the PPV 6h >68.6% 
group demonstrated a significantly reduced need for vasoactive 
drug therapy, with a decrease in mean pump speed, whereas 
the opposite trend was observed in the other group. Regarding 
long-term prognosis, OS in the PPV 6h >68.6% group was sig -
nificantly better, with longer OS times and a significantly lower 
28-day mortality rate. Overall, regardless of shock symptom 
resolution or long-term organ function and overall prognosis, 
patients in the PPV 6h >68.6% group consistently performed 
better, highlighting the potential of this parameter as an indica-
tor of early resuscitation effectiveness.
DISCUSSION
In this prospective single-center cohort study, we systemat -
ically analyzed the clinical data of patients with septic shock, 
and innovatively validated the feasibility of utilizing sublingual 
microcirculation examination as a method to assess the effec -
tiveness of fluid resuscitation. Furthermore, the study proposed 
the potential utility of PPV 6h >68.6% as an endpoint criterion 
for resuscitation and a guide for subsequent treatment.
The 2021 version of the SSC guidelines does not specify a 
clear endpoint for resuscitation (24). However, the central con-
cept underlying this approach is “balance,” encompassing both 
adequacy (restoration of tissue perfusion) and nonexcessive -
ness (disappearance of volume responsiveness and avoidance 
of over-resuscitation). In current first-line treatment practices 
for septic shock, ICU physicians habitually use macrocircula -
tory indicators such as blood pressure and heart rate, as well 
as microcirculation-related variables derived from blood gas 
analysis, including blood lactate levels, ScvO2, and Pcv-aCO2, 
to guide resuscitation. This practice is problematic. Prior stud -
ies have demonstrated that normalization of macrocirculatory 
indicators does not necessarily indicate improvement in micro-
circulatory tissue perfusion or oxygenation status ( 26–30), 
consistent with our findings presented in Figure 1. The pres -
ence of this asynchrony underscores the necessity of incorpo -
rating microcirculatory monitoring into the determination of 
resuscitation endpoints. Previous literature has explored vari -
ous techniques for assessing microcirculation in septic shock, 
including sublingual microcirculation based on SDF technol -
ogy ( 31,32), near-infrared spectroscopy ( 33), transcutaneous 
oxygen measurement and oxygen challenge tests (OCTs) (34), 
laser Doppler flowmetry combined with visible light spectros -
copy ( 35), blood oxygen saturation-derived perfusion index, 
and physical examination-based indicators such as capillary 
refill time, skin mottling, and skin temperature changes ( 36). 
However, most of these studies have primarily focused on the 
correlation between changes in microcirculatory variables and 
patient outcomes, with limited application to guiding treatment.
[[p10]]
170 SHOCK Vol. 65, No. 2 Zhang et al.
Although the data in Tables 1 and 2 confirm the predic -
tive value of PPV 6h and MFI 6h for 28-day prognosis and post -
resuscitation organ dysfunction progression, this was not the 
primary objective of our study. Our aim is to demonstrate 
the potential of sublingual microcirculation as an auxiliary 
tool for determining whether the endpoint of fluid resusci -
tation has been achieved, thereby facilitating its integration 
into treatment guidance. To achieve maximal clinical bene -
fit, we determined the optimal threshold of PPV 6h by anchor -
ing it to three key aspects: tissue oxygenation (Lac 24h), organ 
dysfunction progression ( △APACHE II3d and △SOFA3d), and 
long-term outcomes (adverse prognosis and 28-day mortality). 
This multifaceted anchoring approach represents a significant 
distinction between our study and prior investigations, reflect -
ing our emphasis on evaluating the effectiveness of resuscita -
tion. The derived resuscitation endpoint judgment condition 
(PPV6h > 68.6%) possesses several distinct advantages: (1) it 
requires only a single time-point measurement, thereby min -
imizing interference from heterogeneous baseline values; (2) 
as a relative value index, it exhibits high stability and excellent 
generalizability across devices and analysis software from var-
ious manufacturers; and (3) it demonstrates strong predictive 
performance for both short-term and long-term outcomes. The 
continuous decline of PPV in sepsis patients is associated with 
various pathophysiological changes during the progression of 
septic shock, including microvascular obstruction caused by 
microemboli formation, increased vascular permeability due to 
proinflammatory mediators leading to tissue edema and subse-
quent compression of microcirculatory blood flow, decreased 
vascular tone induced by impaired oxygen utilization or aci -
dosis in tissue cells, and compensatory redistribution of blood 
flow resulting in reduced perfusion in peripheral areas such as 
the sublingual region. Collectively, these findings indicate that 
PPV may serve as a valuable biomarker for evaluating both the 
severity of the condition and the effectiveness of resuscitation 
therapy.
The prognostic predictive efficacy of sublingual microcir -
culation variables measured at baseline was low in this study, 
with no positive results, which contrasts with similar experi -
ments ( 37–39). The research team hypothesized that exclud -
ing patients with severe cardiac dysfunction may explain this 
discrepancy. In some patients, septic shock is caused by acute 
cardiac injury leading to pump failure and secondary organ 
hypoperfusion ( 40,41). Here, microcirculatory failure stems 
from macrocirculatory insufficiency, and parameter abnormal-
ities directly reflect cardiac dysfunction, strongly correlating 
with prognosis. In contrast, most true septic shock cases involve 
microcirculatory disorders, including endothelial dysfunction 
with contractile impairment and permeability damage. These 
abnormalities are heterogeneously distributed, weakening the 
correlation between SDF measurements and disease severity. 
Excluding sepsis-related cardiac dysfunction in this study fur -
ther reduced this correlation, resulting in negative ROC curve 
findings.
A similar phenomenon was observed for blood lactate levels 
in this study. The predictive efficacy of Lac pre and ΔLac% at 
multiple time points for 28-day prognosis was low, while post-
resuscitation Lac absolute values showed increasing predictive 
efficacy over time, peaking at Lac 24h. This may be attributed 
to two factors: First, the lag in Lac changes due to the balance 
between production and metabolism ( 42–44). Despite rapid 
macrocirculatory improvement with fluid resuscitation, organ 
oxygen uptake recovery remains heterogeneous, typically 
reaching equilibrium within 24 hours, which explains Lac 24h’s 
strong prognostic value ( 45–47). Second, ICU patient comor -
bidities introduce variability in factors like fluid infusion rates, 
vasoactive drug pump speeds, and shock treatment timing, 
reducing baseline Lac’s correlation with prognosis. Notably, by 
24 hours postresuscitation, if microcirculatory recovery has not 
occurred, the condition is likely irreversible (48).
In our future research, while the threshold of PPV6h >68.6% 
derived from this study could potentially serve as a guiding indi-
cator for clinical treatment of septic shock, there are still debatable 
aspects remain in such experiments, and their initiation should 
be cautious. To date, numerous clinical trials have explored 
microcirculation-guided treatments for shock (20); however, few 
have yielded positive outcomes. For instance, Raphael et al. (49) 
incorporated sublingual microcirculation monitoring into the 
discussion of treatment decisions alongside existing therapeutic 
strategies. Despite being a large-scale prospective randomized 
controlled trial (N = 141), no significant effects were observed 
on 24-hour microcirculation variables, macrocirculatory vari -
ables, perfusion indices, or 30-day mortality. Nevertheless, the 
intervention group exhibited more refined adjustments in fluid 
resuscitation and vasoactive drug administration. Athanasios et 
al. developed individualized care protocols based on sublingual 
microcirculation phenotypes (N = 13) ( 50), employing inter -
ventions such as blood transfusions, albumin supplementation, 
and CO-based vasodilators to optimize hemodynamics in septic 
shock patients undergoing emergency abdominal surgery. While 
these measures enhanced sublingual microcirculation variables 
intraoperatively, their impact on long-term prognosis remains 
unclear due to the absence of a control group. Notably, despite 
lower 30-day and 60-day mortality rates compared with histori-
cal data, definitive conclusions cannot be drawn without proper 
comparative analysis. Collectively, existing studies utilizing  
microcirculation-guided approaches for septic shock treatment 
have yet to achieve their primary objective of significantly 
improving patient outcomes. The purported short-term enhance-
ments in microcirculation have not been shown to significantly 
impact the overall disease progression, thereby highlighting con-
cerns that necessitate meticulous evaluation in future research.
In the planned randomized controlled trial focusing on  
target-oriented treatment with PPV 6h >68.6%, a key challenge 
is developing a treatment plan for patients who do not meet 
this criterion after 6 hours of resuscitation (51,52). The current 
guidelines recommend early fluid resuscitation; however, the 
optimal rate and total volume of fluid administration remain 
subjects of ongoing debate (23). In practice, this upper limit can 
be determined using volume loading tests or cardiac ultrasound 
to assess the inferior vena cava width. However, if septic shock 
patients who have timely completed the 1-hour and 6-hour 
bundle therapy still exhibit unresolved symptoms, this may be 
attributed to scattered endothelial dysfunction and tissue meta-
bolic alterations (loss of oxygen uptake and utilization capacity) 
(53–59). At this stage, fluid resuscitation and vasoactive drug 
[[p11]]

administration alone are unlikely to yield significant benefits. 
Instead, they may exacerbate conditions through mechanisms 
such as further contraction of small blood vessels, reduced 
tissue perfusion, excessive fluid overload leading to cardiac 
dysfunction, and endothelial barrier disruption causing fluid 
leakage and ineffective circulating volume, ultimately wors -
ening multiorgan edema ( 60,61). Although no definitive stud -
ies provide clear guidance or validated treatments, prior study 
failures highlight important lessons ( 49,62,63). Early identifi -
cation of shock progression via microcirculatory “indicators” 
(such as skin temperature, urine output, and mental status) and 
ultrasound-based assessment of shock types to refine resusci -
tation strategies may contribute to reducing the proportion of 
patients exhibiting ineffective responses.
Our study has several limitations. First, the project was initi-
ated at an early stage, and the guidelines it followed have since 
been updated. Although the new guidelines align with many of 
our management measures, this may still affect the generaliz -
ability of our findings. Second, we excluded patients with heart 
function impairments during the initial inclusion phase, includ-
ing those with sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy, which restricts 
the applicability of our conclusions to patients with sepsis- 
related cardiac dysfunction. Finally, although we calculated 
potential conditions for sublingual microcirculation-guided 
treatment based on the collected data, we were unable to con -
duct clinical interventions to validate these findings.
CONCLUSION
This single-center randomized cohort study examines the 
correlation between Sublingual microcirculation in septic 
shock patients and postresuscitation blood lactate levels, pro -
gression of organ dysfunction, and adverse clinical outcomes. 
It highlights the potential utility of sublingual microcircula -
tion as an adjunctive tool for reflecting the effectiveness of 
resuscitation therapy and proposes that the PPV 6h >68.6% 
may serve as a target for early goal-directed therapy in future 
studies.
