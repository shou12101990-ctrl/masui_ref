A B S T R A C T
Transseptal puncture (TSP) is a critical step in left atrial appendage closure. This consensus document reviews the evolution of TSP techniques and 
instrumentation, comparing conventional mechanical methods with radiofrequency-assisted approaches. It highlights the importance of imaging-based 
preplanning with transoesophageal echocardiography and multislice computed tomography to optimize TSP positioning and procedural outcomes. 
The inferoposterior fossa ovalis position is generally preferred to achieve coaxial device alignment and enhance safety and efficacy, whereas alternative TSP 
positions may suit better specific complex anatomies. Procedural guidance under transoesophageal echocardiography and intracardiac echocardiography-
guidedosto TSP is illustrated, and a stepwise strategy for challenging cases is outlined. We propose an algorithm for TSP preplanning and intraprocedural 
guidance, along with expert consensus recommendations developed during the second European Left Atrial Appendage Club meeting.
Introduction
Left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) is performed to reduce the 
thromboembolic risk in patients with atrial fibrillation. Currently avail-
able percutaneous LAAC devices are delivered via transvenous 
femoral access, crossing the interatrial septum (IAS) into the left atrium 
and appendage. Transseptal puncture (TSP) is a pivotal step in LAAC, 
requiring deliberate planning and execution to achieve safe and well-
aligned device deployment within the native left atrial appendage 
(LAA) anatomy.
In this study, we review  (1) the available  devices  for TSP, (2) the 
importance  and methodology  of preprocedural  planning,  (3) 
intraprocedural  imaging  guidance,  and (4) strategies  for chal-
lenging  scenarios.  We also summarize  consensus  statements  dis-
cussed  at the second  European  Left Atrial  Appendage  Club
meeting  and propose  an algorithm  for systematic  TSP preplanning  
and performance.
Currently available devices for TSP
TSP has been performed for several decades in various interven-
tional settings, including electrophysiology, transcatheter valve ther-
apies for left-sided heart valve disease, and LAAC procedures. Despite 
its application in these highly specialized procedures, the basic tech-
nique and standard TSP instrumentation have remained unchanged for 
many years. A classical TSP for LAAC is performed by applying me-
chanical pressure to a sharp needle at the right atrial side of the IAS.

Preplanning of TSP
Imaging-based preplanning of LAAC procedures is essential to 
assess technical feasibility, identify anatomical challenges, and define 
procedural strategies. During this preplanning of the LAAC procedure, 
special attention should be paid to the TSP strategy, which can be 
performed using transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and/or 
multislice computed tomography (MSCT). Both modalities provide a 3-
dimension (3D) anatomical understanding of the fossa ovalis within the 
IAS, the LAA, and their orientation relative to each other. TEE allows 
accurate assessment of the size and mobility of the fossa ovalis and the 
presence of patent foramen ovale or any other septal defects while 
also providing insight into the size of both atria. Although MSCT 
provides less information about these intrinsic IAS challenges, it can 
also identify septal defects and offers valuable information on the IAS 
and fossa orientation within the thorax and relative to the LAA.
Tailored positioning of the TSP site is crucial for optimizing the 
efficacy, safety, and procedural simplicity of LAAC procedures. The 
TSP site should be strategically positioned to enable direct and coaxial 
engagement with the LAA. Proper alignment of the LAAC device with 
the native anatomical axis of the LAA is essential to ensure complete 
appendage occlusion, minimizing the risk of peridevice leakage and 
potential thrombus formation. Peridevice leakage is more frequently 
associated with not only undersized devices but also noncoaxial 
deployment of the LAAC device, whereas the risk of device thrombosis 
is higher with deep device implantation. 2,3 Both are considered risk 
factors for ischemic events and should therefore be avoided. In addi-
tion, the more coaxially the LAA delivery system engages the 
appendage, the easier it is to implant the LAAC device, reducing the 
need for device recapture and/or septal repuncture. Easier implanta-
tion can be argued to improve safety and reduce radiation and 
contrast exposure. The introduction of newly developed steerable 
sheaths can help finetune device alignment, but steerability cannot 
compensate for significant TSP location offsets.
The optimal location of the TSP depends on the spatial relationship 
between the fossa, the ostial LAA location, and the proximal LAA 
orientation. Creating a virtual grid within the fossa along the anterior-
posterior and inferior-superior axes is used to define the optimal TSP 
during imaging-based preplanning and can be translated during the 
imaging-guided TSP itself.
Studies suggest that a typical inferior and posterior TSP is ideal for 
45% to 80% of LAAC procedures. 4–7 However, for anatomies with a 
more posteriorly oriented proximal LAA axis (eg, “reverse chicken-w-
ing”), a more central or even anterior TSP location is advised (Figure 2). 
Conversely, a superior TSP location is rarely preferred. 4–7 In line with 
this, the use of an incidental patent foramen ovale as an access route

A stepwise MSCT-based approach to determine the optimal TSP 
position was described by Fukutomi et al, 4 assessing anterior-posterior 
and superior-inferior positions using lateral (RAO) and apical (LAO) 
angiographic views of the left atrium (Figure 3). In the LAO view, the 
fossa’s superior and inferior borders become separated, whereas the 
RAO view delineates its anterior and posterior borders. In their series, 
a posterior TSP was favored in ~75% of cases, whereas central and 
anterior punctures (17% and 8%, respectively) were mainly required in 
anatomies where the proximal bend of the LAA was oriented poste-
riorly, such as in the so-called “reverse chicken-wing” configuration. 
MSCT analysis can be performed with any software supporting 
multiplanar reconstruction, as is shown in Figure 4. As a first step, 
scrolling from anterior to posterior while observing the axial, coronal, 
and sagittal planes helps determine the shape and orientation of the 
LAA. Next, the crosshair is positioned at the LAA ostium, with one plane 
passing through the coumadin ridge and another through the circum-
flex artery, which lies anterior of the LAA. In this view, the proximal LAA 
axis can be defined by connecting the center of the LAA ostium to the 
center of the LAA landing zone, either being predominantly anterior 
(<90 ◦ ) or posterior (≥90 ◦ ). Alternatively, dedicated MSCT software for 
LAAC preplanning, such as 3Mensio (3Mensio Medical Imaging BV), 
TruPlan (Boston Scientific), or FEops (FEops/Materialise), can facilitate 
this process. These platforms allow easy visualization of the fossa ovalis 
and LAA axis, assessment of their spatial relationship in LAO and RAO 
projections, and simulation of delivery sheath trajectories from different 
puncture sites to identify the optimal TSP location.
As MSCT may be contraindicated  for contrast usage or may not be 
readily available due to limited resources, waiting lists, or reim-
bursement policies, TEE can also be used to assess and determine 
the optimal TSP position. A combination of 2-dimension and 3D TEE 
images distinguishes  the anatomy of the fossa ovalis, the shape and 
position of the appendage, and its orientation.  3D TEE views that 
help determine the optimal TSP position are shown in Figure 5. The 
optimal TSP position is determined based on 3D reconstructions  that 
include the fossa, the left atrium, and the LAA. First, a 3D view of the 
anatomical spatial relationship between the fossa and the LAA ostium 
within the left atrium is visualized, and second, the orientation of the 
proximal neck of the LAA is revealed by “cutting” through the roof of 
the appendage. The latter 2, like MSCT preplanning,  show that a 
more posterior position of the TSP is favorable in the case of 
an anterior position of the LAA and/or an anterior proximal bend 
of the LAA.
Both MSCT and TEE provide the 3D information needed to assess 
the spatial relationship between the TSP location and appendage 
orientation. The consensus of the meeting was that at least 1 form of 
3D multiplanar reconstruction—either 3D TEE or MSCT—is essential 
for preplanning LAAC, including TSP positioning and LAA sizing. A 
structured algorithm for this analysis should be developed for both 
imaging modalities.
Consensus statement for preplanning of TSP
• Image-based preplanning of LAAC procedures is recommended 
to preemptively assess technical feasibility and challenges and 
define the preferred procedural strategy.
• Preplanning should include at least 1 3D imaging modality, such 
as MSCT or 3D TEE, to accurately assess the fossa ovalis, the 
LAA, and their orientation relative to each other.
• The location of the TSP should be tailored to optimize alignment 
between the LAAC delivery catheter and the native LAA 
anatomical axis, thereby improving safety and reducing proce-
dural complexity.
• An inferior TSP position is recommended for all LAAC 
procedures.
• A posterior TSP position is preferred for the majority of LAAC 
procedures. However, a mid-to anterior TSP position should be 
considered if the proximal neck of the LAA is directed more 
posteriorly.
Intraprocedural TSP imaging guidance
In general, TSP for LAAC procedures is recommended to be per-
formed under ultrasound and fluoroscopic guidance for (1) routing 
toward the intended TSP position and (2) tracking needle movements 
(eg, fossa perforation and position in the left atrium). The most com-
mon practice is the use of a standard TEE probe in general anesthesia. 
The smaller introducer profiles of mini-and micro-TEE probes allow 
LAAC procedures to be performed under local anesthesia with 
conscious sedation. Modern types of mini-or micro-TEE probes also 
allow the construction of qualitative multiplanar views. Alternatively, 
LAAC can be performed using intracardiac echo (ICE). The advantage 
of using ICE is that it creates the possibility to perform LAAC under 
local anesthesia and serves as a valuable alternative when esophageal 
intubation is contraindicated. The ICE probe is introduced via an 8-10

Consensus statement for intraprocedural TSP imaging 
guidance
• TSP in LAAC procedures should be performed under real-time 
fluoroscopic and ultrasound imaging (TEE or ICE) to minimize 
complications and ensure a targeted TSP.
• Standardized imaging protocols for TEE and ICE should be 
developed and adopted to ensure a consistent and stepwise TSP 
in LAAC procedures.
Strategies in complex TSP scenarios
Several intrinsic and extrinsic factors can complicate TSP (Table 1). 
Extrinsic factors alter the orientation of the IAS or its pathway toward 
the fossa ovalis, affecting the spatial relation between cardiac and 
noncardiac structures and hindering access and orientation of the
TSP kit at the fossa ovalis. Intrinsic factors, on the other hand, influ-
ence the transseptal crossing due to specific characteristics  of the 
fossa itself. Although some of these complicating  factors can be 
identified during preplanning based on TEE and/or MSCT imaging, 
specific challenges during TSP are not always as easy to predict. 
Therefore, it is important to follow a stepwise approach to address 
unexpected challenges during the procedure and have a basic toolkit 
available.
In cases with intrinsic challenges such as a floppy, thickened, or 
fibrotic septum, achieving anatomical precision during TSP can be 
challenging.  This may lead to uncontrolled  penetration forces, which 
can result in adverse safety events. Pressure monitoring or a radi-
opaque coronary wire inside the TSP needle can help identify when 
the tip of the needle crosses the septum into the left atrium. At this 
point, a coronary wire can be advanced through the needle and 
guided toward the left upper pulmonary vein. Alternatively, the stiff 
back end of a coronary wire or the TSP stylet can be used to puncture

• A consistent stepwise protocol and toolkit should be available to 
tackle challenging TSP in LAAC procedures.
Limitations
The recommendations presented herein were initially formulated at 
the first European Left Atrial Appendage Club meeting in 2024 and 
subsequently refined through expert discussion at the 2025 meeting. 
They represent the collective consensus of experienced operators 
rather than evidence-based guidelines, as current data remain insuf-
ficient to confirm a causal relationship between TSP positioning, LAAC 
safety, and closure efficacy. This consensus therefore serves as a 
foundation for future studies designed to investigate these relation-
ships in a systematic and evidence-driven manner.
Conclusion
TSP is a commonly performed procedure, with reliable outcomes 
using the traditional tools. Novel technologies aim to facilitate TSP and 
improve its anatomical precision. Tailoring the position of the TSP in 
LAAC procedures is crucial for achieving coaxial engagement and 
deployment within the native LAA anatomy, potentially improving 
safety and efficacy while reducing procedural complexity.
An algorithm is proposed (Central Illustration) for (1) preplanning 
by first identifying factors that can complicate TSP and second 
defining the optimal TSP position and (2) intraprocedural  imaging 
guidance for correct TSP position and a stepwise technical approach 
in case of challengingS  TSP anatomies. Validation of this algorithm is 
required.
