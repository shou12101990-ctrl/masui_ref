ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Right ventricle–pulmonary artery (RV-PA) conduit stenosis is a relatively uncommon complication after
a childhood Ross-Konno procedure.
CASE SUMMARY A man in his 20s presented with progressive dyspnea for 1 year. He had previously undergone modi ﬁed
Konno and Ross-Konno procedures for congenital subaortic stenosis followed by pulmonic balloon valvuloplasty for RV-PA
stenosis. Echocardiography demonstrated reduced left ventricular ejection fraction, severe pulmonary hypertension, and
right ventricular out ﬂow tract obstruction. Cardiac computed tomography revealed right ventricular out ﬂow/conduit
stenosis with calci ﬁcation. Right heart catheterization showed elevated right ventricular out ﬂow/conduit pressure
gradient, conﬁrming severe conduit stenosis. He was referred for surgical pulmonic valve and conduit repair.
DISCUSSION Surgical conduit replacement remains the most de ﬁnitive treatment strategy in small, calci ﬁed conduits.
Multimodal imaging strategies aid in comprehensive assessment of anatomy and function of RV-PA conduit and valves to
guide intervention strategies in adults with complex congenital heart disease. (JACC Case Rep. 2025;30:104341)

INVESTIGATIONS
A 12-lead electrocardiogram showed normal sinus
rhythm with right axis deviation. Blood in-
vestigations were within normal limits. A
transthoracic echocardiog raphy (TTE) obtained at this
center found a reduced left ventricular ejection frac-
tion (35%-40%), reduced right ventricular systolic
function, dilated right atrium, and coronary sinus with
severe pulmonary hypertension ( Figure 1 , Video 1 ).
Transesophageal echocardiography con ﬁrmed a mild-
moderate decline in left ven tricular ejection fraction
(40%-45%) and demonstrated moderate tricuspid
regurgitation, elevated right ventricular systolic
pressure ( >60 mm Hg) suggestive of signi ﬁcant ﬂow
obstruction at or below the pulmonic valve, and
poorly visualized pulmonic valve lea ﬂets ( Videos 2
to 4 ). Cardiac computed tomography (CT) performed
to better delineate the location and anatomy of the
obstruction showed hyperdensities in right ventric-
ular out ﬂow and pulmonic annulus suggestive of
conduit calci ﬁcation and narrowing (1.2-1.3 cm) fol-
lowed by post-stenotic dilatation of the main pul-
monary artery trunk (2.9 cm) ( Figure 2 ). Although the
pulmonic valve was not visualized in its entirety,
leaﬂets appeared to be mobile and open without re-
striction. The right atrium and ventricle were dilated
with reduced right ventricular systolic function
(ejection fraction 41%). Left ventricle, aortic valve,
mitral valve, and pericardium were unremarkable. No
evidence of atherosclerosis or obstruction in coro-
naries was detected. The patient underwent right
heart catheterization for invasive hemodynamic
assessment of the right ventricle, right ventricular
outﬂow/conduit, and pulmonary artery pressures
(ﬁndings summarized in Table 1 ). Fluoroscopy after
injection of contrast agent showed mild-moderate
calci ﬁcation in right ventricular out ﬂow/conduit,
and angiography revealed a signi ﬁcantly smaller
caliber of right ventricular out ﬂow/conduit (10.6 mm)
compared with distal pulmonary artery trunk

surgical conduit replacement as the preferred

especially when prior percutaneous interventions
have failed or are deemed high risk due to

defer repeat balloon angioplasty and proceed
with surgical repair re ﬂects a multidisciplinary
consensus incorporating multimodal imaging ﬁnd-
ings, prior procedural history, and unique anatomic
complexities. This case illustrates the importance of
an individualized, anatomy -driven, multimodal im-
aging strategy for conduit dysfunction after Ross-
Konno procedures, particularly in adult patients
with small, calci ﬁed conduits and reduced ventric-
ular reserve.
CONCLUSIONS
RV-PA conduit dysfunction is associated with smaller
conduit diameter, male sex, younger patient and
donor age, and use of bioprostheses. TTE is the initial
diagnostic tool for RVOT and conduit evaluation,
whereas cardiac CT and MRI provide detailed
anatomic and functional assessment. A multimodal
imaging strategy for delineation of anatomy and
function of conduit, valves, ventricles, and pulmo-
nary arteries supplemented by invasive cardiac
catheterization for hemodynamic assessment is
crucial to guide the intervention strategy in complex
congenital heart diseases in adults.
