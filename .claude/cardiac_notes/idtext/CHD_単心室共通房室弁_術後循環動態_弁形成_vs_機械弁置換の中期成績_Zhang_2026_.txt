ABSTRACT
Objectives To evaluate the mid- term outcomes 
of mechanical valve replacement of the common 
atrioventricular valve (CAVV) in single- ventricle pediatric 
patients and compare this with valvuloplasty.
Methods We conducted a retrospective study of 91 
single- ventricle pediatric patients who underwent 
CAVV surgery between 2014 and 2025. Patients were 
divided into two groups according to the initial surgery: 
valvuloplasty and mechanical valve replacement groups. 
Baseline and perioperative characteristics were compared 
between groups. Overall survival and freedom from 
reintervention were analyzed using Kaplan- Meier curves. 
Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and 
maximally selected rank statistics were used to determine 
the optimal cut- off values for cardiopulmonary bypass 
(CPB) and aortic cross- clamp (ACC) time in predicting in- 
hospital and late mortality.
Results There were 78 cases in valvuloplasty and 13 
cases in the mechanical valve replacement group. The 
replacement group had significantly longer CPB and ACC 
time (p<0.001). In- hospital mortality was 2 out of 13 
(15.4%) in the replacement group and 8 out of 78 (10.3%) 
in the valvuloplasty group (p>0.050). The median follow- 
up was 39 months. Kaplan- Meier analysis showed no 
significant difference in overall survival (p=0.280) and 
freedom from reintervention (p=0.270). Reintervention for 
recurrent regurgitation was required in eight patients after 
ventriculoplasty, whereas no reintervention was observed 
following valve replacement. ROC analysis identified a CPB 
time of 173.5 min as the optimal cut- off for predicting 
in- hospital mortality,with an area under the curve (AUC) of 
0.751. Patients with CPB duration exceeding 180 min had 
significantly poorer survival (p=0.003).
Conclusions Mechanical valve replacement of the CAVV 
in single- ventricle pediatric patients provides satisfactory 
mid- term survival comparable to valvuloplasty, but with 
superior valve durability. Prolonged CPB time is associated 
with poorer survival and should be minimized whenever 
possible.

CAVV regurgitation, with moderate to severe 
regurgitation occurring in approximately 
one- fifth of cases. 2 Indeed, CAVV regurgita-
tion is a well- recognized risk factor for adverse

long- term survival.1 4  Effective management of CAVV

treatment of this complex population. Although valve 
plasty has long been the preferred strategy in order to 
preserve native valve tissue and avoid prosthesis- related 
complications, its long- term durability has been subop-
timal.1 5 6
Over the past decade, refinements in surgical tech-
niques and perioperative management have led to signif-
icant improvements in the outcomes of CAVV repair. 5 
Nevertheless, recurrent valve regurgitation continues to 
represent a substantial clinical problem, often compro-
mising long- term survival and functional recovery.7 Early 
reports suggested that mechanical valve replacement was 
associated with high operative mortality and limited mid- 
term survival, which may have limited its use in single- 
ventricle patients. 8 However, more recent studies have 
indicated that with improved surgical experience and 
postoperative care, mechanical valve replacement can 
provide acceptable early outcomes and durable valve 
function. This represents a reasonable alternative in 
cases with severely dysplastic or irreparable valves.9
Given these issues, the optimal surgical strategy 
for managing CAVV regurgitation in single- ventricle 
patients remains controversial. The present study aimed 
to evaluate the mid- term outcomes of mechanical valve 
replacement for the CAVV and compare them with those 
of valvuloplasty. In addition, we aim to identify poten-
tial perioperative factors associated with early and late 
mortality.
PATIENTS AND METHODS
Study design and patient population
This retrospective study included single- ventricle pedi-
atric patients who underwent surgery involving CAVV 
repair or replacement at Fuwai Hospital, Chinese 
Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical 
College, Beijing, China, between December 2014 and 
September 2025. All patients had a definitive diagnosis of 
single- ventricle physiology with significant CAVV regurgi-
tation requiring surgical intervention. Complete periop-
erative and follow- up data were available for all patients.
According to the initial surgical strategy, patients were 
divided into two groups: those who underwent CAVV 
valvuloplasty (Group- Plasty) and those who underwent 
mechanical valve replacement (Group- Replacement). 
For patients who underwent reoperation during the 
same hospitalization, only the initial surgical procedure 
was considered for analysis.
Surgical strategy
The choice between CAVV valvuloplasty and mechanical 
valve replacement was determined by the intraoperative 
assessment of valve morphology, leaflet mobility, and the 
severity of regurgitation. Valvuloplasty was attempted 
whenever the native leaflets and sub- valvular apparatus 
were deemed suitable for reconstruction and when satis-
factory valve competence could be achieved after repair. 
Various repair techniques were applied according to the 
specific valve pathology, including edge- to- edge repair, 
annuloplasty, cleft closure, commissural suturing, resec-
tion of abnormal subvalvular chordae, and use of the 
bivalvation technique. Detailed surgical techniques have 
been described previously.10
Mechanical valve replacement was performed when 
the CAVV was judged irreparable due to severe leaflet 
dysplasia, extensive chordal fusion, or marked annular 
distortion, or when adequate coaptation could not be 
obtained despite repeated repair attempts. In patients 
undergoing staged single- ventricle palliation, valve 
replacement was also considered if severe regurgitation 
was expected to compromise ventricular function or the 
subsequent Fontan circulation. Valve size selection was 
based on annular diameter and patient body surface 
area to ensure unobstructed inflow without significant 
mismatch. For patients receiving a mechanical valve, 
lifelong anticoagulation with warfarin was initiated post-
operatively. Target international normalized ratio was 
maintained between 2.0 and 2.5, and anticoagulation was 
regularly monitored and adjusted to remain in this ther -
apeutic range.
Data collection
Clinical data were retrieved from the institutional infor -
mation center. Baseline characteristics included age, sex, 
weight, and height, as well as anatomical and physiolog-
ical features such as ventricular dominance, atrial isom-
erism, and major cardiac malformations. Preoperative 
hemodynamic status was assessed using right heart cath-
eterization data, including pulmonary artery pressure 
(PAP) and pulmonary artery development (quantified 
using the Nakata index).
Intraoperative variables comprised the performance of 
concomitant procedures, cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) 
time, aortic cross- clamp (ACC) time, total duration of 
surgery, and the use of blood transfusion, including 
transfusion volume.
Postoperative outcomes included ejection fraction 
(EF), duration of mechanical ventilation, length of stay 
in the intensive care unit (ICU), total postoperative 
hospital stay, and in- hospital mortality.
Follow- up data were obtained from outpatient records 
and telephone interviews. The primary endpoints were 
overall survival. The secondary endpoint was freedom 
from valve reintervention. For patients who underwent 
initial valvuloplasty, valve failure was defined as the recur-
rence of moderate or greater CAVV regurgitation or the 
need for reoperation on the valve.
Statistical analysis
Continuous variables were expressed as the mean±-
standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range 
(IQR)), as appropriate, and compared using the Student’s

t- test or Mann- Whitney U test. Paired comparisons of 
continuous variables (e.g., preoperative and postopera-

or Wilcoxon signed- rank test, as appropriate. Categorical 
variables were presented as frequencies and percentages 
and compared using the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test. Univar-
iable Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was 
performed to identify factors associated with mortality.
Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve anal-
ysis was performed to determine the predictive ability 
of continuous intraoperative variables (CPB time and 
ACC time) for in- hospital death. Optimal cut- off points 
were identified using the maximally selected rank statis-
tics method, which provided threshold values that maxi-
mized separation between survival groups.
Kaplan- Meier survival curves were generated to esti-
mate overall survival and freedom from reintervention. 
Heart transplantation was considered as an endpoint 
equivalent to death in survival analyses. The log- rank 
test was used to compare survival distributions between 
Group- Plasty and Group- Replacement and between 
patients above and below the ROC- derived cut- off values. 
A two- sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically 
significant.
All statistical analyses were performed using R software 
(V.4.3.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, 
Austria).

Perioperative characteristics
Perioperative characteristics are presented in table 2. 
The mean CPB time (225.0 vs. 153.1 min; p<0.001) and

DISCUSSION
In this retrospective cohort of 91 pediatric patients with 
functional single- ventricle physiology undergoing their 
first operation for CAVV regurgitation, we made several 
important observations. First, the majority of patients 
underwent valvuloplasty, while mechanical valve replace-
ment was reserved for a smaller subset. Despite differ -
ences in baseline characteristics including age, body size, 
and pulmonary artery development, overall survival was 
comparable between the two strategies during a median 
follow- up of 39 months. Second, valvuloplasty demon-
strated limited long- term durability, with all recurrent 
dysfunction and reinterventions occurring exclusively 
in this group; no reintervention was required after 
valve replacement. Third, prolonged CPB duration was 
strongly associated with increased late mortality, while 
ACC time showed a weaker, non- significant relationship. 
These results highlight the distinct clinical profiles of 
patients selected for each surgical approach and under -
score the prognostic significance of perioperative factors, 
particularly CPB duration, in this high- risk population. 
The relatively high in- hospital mortality observed in this 
study likely reflects the complexity of single- ventricle 
physiology, as many patients had significant associated 
anomalies and required concomitant complex proce-
dures. These include TAPVC repair or Fontan comple-
tion, which may have substantially increased perioper -
ative risk. Single- center series have reported similarly 
high early postoperative mortality following CAVV 
surgery in patients with single- ventricle physiology, with 
rates reaching up to 23.3%, highlighting the inherently 
elevated surgical risk in this population.11
Previous studies have consistently demonstrated that 
the long- term durability of CAVV valvuloplasty in single-  
ventricle patients remains suboptimal, with progressive 
regurgitation and recurrent dysfunction frequently 
reported during follow- up.1 Our results indicate that 
moderate or greater residual CAVV regurgitation after 
surgery is strongly associated with increased mortality. 
This finding emphasizes the clinical importance of 
achieving optimal valve function during valvuloplasty, as 
residual dysfunction may lead to chronic volume over -
load and progressive ventricular deterioration in single-  
ventricle physiology. These observations align with 
previous reports highlighting residual regurgitation as 
a key determinant of long- term outcome. 12 Of note, 
compared with our institution’s previous cohort from 
2007 to 2018, in which the 5- year freedom from valve 
failure after valvuloplasty reached 72.7%, 10 the contem-
porary cohort in the present study demonstrated a 
substantially lower rate of 50.7%. This suggests that the 
long- term durability of valvuloplasty has not improved 
in recent years. It is important to note, however, that 
this apparent decline may be influenced by changes in

all valve- related reinterventions in the present cohort 
occurred exclusively after valvuloplasty, while no rein-

observe evidence of early deterioration in ventricular 
function following valve replacement. In the present 
study, preoperative EF and EF before discharge were 
comparable, suggesting that mechanical valve implanta-
tion did not adversely affect ventricular performance in 
appropriately selected patients. While early postopera-
tive EF was preserved, the lack of longitudinal follow- up 
data limits assessment of long- term ventricular function 
after valve replacement. Collectively, these observations 
support the superior durability of mechanical valve 
replacement compared with repair in carefully selected 
patients with CAVV regurgitation.
Patients selected for valve replacement were signifi-
cantly older and had greater body weight, height, and 
body surface area, reflecting the practical limitation 
that implantation of a mechanical prosthesis requires a 
minimum annular size. This consideration is supported 
by prior work from Sugimoto et al., who reported signifi-
cantly higher mortality among patients receiving pros-

our cohort, all patients undergoing CAVV replacement 
received mechanical prostheses with a diameter of at

observed in the Replacement group. Patient selection 
for valvuloplasty vs valve replacement remains a key chal-
lenge in the management of CAVV regurgitation. In the 
present study, patients undergoing valve replacement 
were older and had larger body size, reflecting the prac-

prosthetic valve implantation. In contrast, valvuloplasty 
was associated with limited durability, and residual regur-
gitation was strongly linked to increased mortality. These 
findings suggest that in patients with unfavorable valve 
morphology or a high likelihood of residual regurgita-
tion, earlier consideration of a more definitive strategy 
may be beneficial. However, given the limitations of 
this study, surgical decision- making should remain 
individualized.
ROC analysis identified a CPB duration of 173.5 min 
as the optimal cut- off for predicting in- hospital mortality 
(AUC=0.751). When stratified using a clinically rele-
vant threshold, patients with CPB ≥180 min demon-
strated significantly worse long- term survival than those 
with shorter CPB duration. Although few prior studies 
have defined specific CPB thresholds in this popula-
tion, prolonged CPB has consistently been associated 
with adverse outcomes in single- ventricle palliation, 
likely mediated by systemic inflammatory activation and 
myocardial injury.13 14 The weaker predictive value of ACC 
time (AUC=0.632) suggests that duration of total circula-
tory support, rather than myocardial ischemic time alone, 
drives early mortality in these complex procedures.
Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. 
First, because functional single- ventricle physiology with 
CAVV regurgitation is a rare condition, the sample size 
was relatively small, which may have limited the statistical 
power to detect modest differences between treatment 
strategies. Second, the retrospective design is inherently 
subject to selection bias, unmeasured confounding, and 
incomplete data capture, despite careful data collec-
tion and follow- up. Third, the limited number of events 
precluded more detailed risk- factor analyses, including 
comprehensive multivariate or regression modeling, 
which may have provided additional insight into predic-
tors of outcomes. Fourth, the median follow- up dura-
tion of 39 months restricts assessment of long- term 
outcomes, particularly valve durability and late survival. 
Longer follow- up is required to fully characterize the 
late consequences of different surgical strategies. Finally, 
the study population consisted predominantly of older 
and larger patients. In clinical practice, CAVV regurgita-
tion is frequently encountered in neonates and infants; 
however, these patients were under- represented in the 
present cohort. Therefore, the findings of this study may 
not be generalizable to younger patients, particularly 
those in the neonatal or early infancy period.
In conclusion, CAVV ventriculoplasty in pediatric 
patients with functional single- ventricle physiology is 
associated with limited long- term durability, and its 
success is highly dependent on achieving optimal valve 
competence. Residual moderate or greater regurgitation 
is strongly associated with increased mortality, under -
scoring the clinical importance of an effective repair. 
In older and larger patients, mechanical valve replace-
ment provides survival outcomes comparable to those of 
valve repair while offering superior freedom from valve 
reintervention, without evidence of early deterioration 
in ventricular function. These findings suggest that, in 
pediatric patients with a high likelihood of residual 
regurgitation or technically challenging valve anatomy, 
earlier consideration of a more definitive strategy may be 
warranted.
Given the strong association between prolonged CPB 
and adverse outcomes, operative strategies should prior -
itize minimizing the duration of bypass. Overall, our 
data highlight the critical role of patient selection in
