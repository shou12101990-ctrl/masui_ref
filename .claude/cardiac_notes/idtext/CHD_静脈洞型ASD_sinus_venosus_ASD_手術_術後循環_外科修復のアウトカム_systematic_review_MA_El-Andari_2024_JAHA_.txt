ORIGINAL RESEARCH
Outcomes Following Surgical Repair of  
Sinus Venosus Atrial Septal Defects: 
A Systematic Review and Meta- Analysis
Ryaan El- Andari , MD*; Muhammad Moolla , MD*; Kevin John , MBBS; Ashley Slingerland , BSc; 
Sandra Campbell , MLS; Jeevan Nagendran , MD; Yongzhe Hong , MD, PhD; Anoop Mathew , MBBS
BACKGROUND: Sinus venosus atrial septal defect (SVASD) is a rare congenital cardiac anomaly comprising 5% to 10% of all 
atrial septal defects. Although surgical closure is the standard treatment for SVASD, data on outcomes have been confined to 
small cohorts. Thus, we conducted a systematic review of the outcomes of SVASD repair.
METHODS AND RESULTS: The primary outcome was death. Secondary outcomes encompassed atrial fibrillation, sinus node 
dysfunction, pacemaker insertion, cerebrovascular accident, reoperation, residual septal defect, superior vena cava ob -
struction, and reimplanted pulmonary vein obstruction. Pooled incidences of outcomes were calculated using a random- 
effects model. Forty studies involving 1320 patients who underwent SVASD repair were included. The majority were male 
patients (55.4%), with 88.0% presenting with associated anomalous pulmonary venous connection. The weighted mean 
age was 18.6±12.5 years, and the overall weighted mean follow-  up period was 8.6±10.4 years. The in-  hospital mortality rate 
was 0.24%, with a 30-  day mortality rate of 0.5% reported in 780 patients. Incidences of atrial fibrillation, sinus node dys -
function, pacemaker insertion, and cerebrovascular accident over the long-  term follow- up were 3.3% (2.18%–4.93%), 6.5% 
(5.09%–8.2%), 2.23% (1.34%–3.57%), and 2.03% (0.89%–2.46%) respectively. Reoperation occurred in 1.36% (0.68%–2.42%) 
of surgeries, residual septal defect in 1.34% (0.69%–2.42%), superior vena cava obstruction in 1.76% (1.02%–2.9%), and re -
implanted pulmonary vein obstruction in 1.4% (0.7%–2.49%).
CONCLUSIONS: This is the first comprehensive analysis of outcomes following surgical repair of SVASD. The findings affirm the 
safety and effectiveness of surgery, establishing a reference point for evaluating emerging transcatheter therapies. Safety and 
efficacy profiles comparable to surgical repair are essential for widespread adoption of transcatheter treatments.

METHODS
As this was a systematic review of existing literature, 
the study did not require review by the institutional re -
view board, and informed consent was not required. 
The data that support the findings of this study are 
available from the corresponding author upon reason -
able request.
Search Criteria
We searched the PROSPERO, MEDLINE, EMBASE, 
and Cochrane Library databases to identify studies. 
Search terms included “sinus venosus,” “ASD,” and 
“atrial septal defect.” We searched the listed data -
bases from 1980 to August 18, 2022. The search was 
restricted to human and English language studies.
Study Selection
We included randomized trials, prospective and retro -
spective case series, and cohort studies that reported 
outcomes of patients with SVASD. Inclusion criteria 
included studies reporting outcome data after surgi -
cal repair of SVASD. Studies not reporting patient 
outcomes, and case reports with ≤2 patients were 
excluded.
All abstracts and titles were screened for inclusion 
by 3 independent reviewers (R.E., K.J., and M.M.). Two 
other reviewers (A.S. and R.E.) screened excluded 
studies to ensure that no studies were incorrectly ex -
cluded. Full- text articles were assessed against inclu -
sion and exclusion criteria by 3 independent reviewers 
(R.E., K.J., and M.M.), with discrepancies resolved by a 
fourth reviewer (A.M.).
Data Extraction
Four reviewers (R.E., A.S., K.J., and M.M.) indepen -
dently extracted pertinent data from full-  text articles, 
and discrepancies in data extraction were resolved by 
consensus. We collected qualitative data on coexist -
ent cardiac lesions, SVASD type, surgical approach, 
and repair technique. The primary outcome was death. 
Secondary outcomes included stroke, atrial fibrillation, 
sinus node dysfunction, pacemaker insertion, reopera-
tion, superior vena cava (SVC) obstruction, reimplanted 
pulmonary vein obstruction, and residual atrial septal 
defect.
Quality Evaluation
The Newcastle–Ottawa Quality Assessment Form for 
Cohort Studies was used to evaluate included studies 
for risk of bias. 11 Each study was judged on 8 items 
across 3 groups: selection and comparability of the 
study groups as well as outcome assessment. Stars 
were awarded for each quality item, and the highest- 
quality studies were awarded up to 9 stars. Risk of bias 
was assessed by 2 reviewers (K.J. and M.M.), with dis-
putes resolved by a third reviewer (A.M.). We catego -
rized risk of bias as high (score of 0–3), moderate (4–6), 
or low (7–9).
CLINICAL PERSPECTIVE
What Is New?
• The landscape for managing sinus venosus 
atrial septal defects has witnessed a dynamic 
evolution, incorporating innovative transcath -
eter techniques alongside traditional surgical 
approaches.
• This systematic review, comprising 40 studies 
and 1320 patients, is the first comprehensive 
review of the outcomes following surgical repair 
of sinus venosus atrial septal defect.
What Are the Clinical Implications?
• Surgical repair of sinus venosus atrial septal 
defects continues to be the established stand -
ard of care, demonstrating excellent short-  term 
outcomes with in- hospital and 30- day mortality 
rates <1%.
• The landscape of surgical management for 
sinus venosus atrial septal defects has rapidly 
evolved, yielding a diverse array of surgical ap -
proaches and techniques across multiple cent -
ers; these varied approaches have consistently 
yielded excellent outcomes, showcasing low 
operative mortality rates and favorable long- 
term results.
• As transcatheter-  based methods for sinus 
venosus atrial septal defect repair emerge, they 
face the imperative to meet or exceed the es-
tablished surgical benchmark, especially re -
garding the long- term risk profile.

Subanalysis of Transcatheter Repair
A systematic review of the literature was conducted 
looking for articles reporting transcatheter repair of 
SVASD. We searched the PROSPERO, MEDLINE, 
EMBASE, and Cochrane Library databases to iden -
tify studies. Search terms included “sinus venosus,” 
“ASD,” and “atrial septal defect.” We searched the 
listed databases from the first reported case of SVASD 
in 2013 (reference paper) to the present. Two reviewers 
screened articles using the same protocols as for the 
initial search.
Statistical Analysis
We presented categorical variables as frequencies and 
percentages, while continuous data were expressed 
as mean±SD or median±interquartile range where ap -
plicable. Given the low event counts in each study, 
using the traditional inverse variance method for meta- 
analysis is not well suited as studies with no events 
will be excluded from the meta-  analysis. We therefore 
adopted the exact inference for fixed-  effects meta- 
analysis for proportions described by Hansen and Rice, 
on the basis of which the estimated pooled incidence, 
95% exact Blaker interval, and delta were calculated.12 
Delta is a quantitative measure of the heterogeneity of 
study- specific proportions. Generally, delta ≤1 is con -
sidered mild heterogeneity; delta >1 and <2 is interme-
diate heterogeneity; delta ≥2 is severe heterogeneity. 
All analyses were performed using Cochrane Review 
Manager (https:tech.cochrane.org/revman) 5.4.1 and 
R Statistical Software version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for 
Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).
RESULTS
Study Selection
Database search yielded 846 studies after removal of 
duplicates ( Figure  1). Five of these studies were ob -
tained from study reference lists. A total of 141 full- text 
articles were retained after the screening of titles and 
abstracts using the exclusion criteria. Full-  text articles 
were reviewed, and 39 studies meeting the inclusion 
criteria were included for qualitative and quantitative 
synthesis6,8,13–49 (Table 1). According to the Newcastle–
Ottawa Scale, no study had a low risk of bias, 39 had 
moderate risk, and 1 had high risk of bias 6,8,13–39,41–49 
(Table S1).
Study Characteristics
Across 40 studies, 1320 patients underwent SVASD 
repair.6,8,13–35,37–41,43–49 Most patients (88.0%) had as -
sociated partial anomalous pulmonary venous con -
nection. A persistent left- sided superior vena cava was 
the second most common associated lesion found in 
79 (20.8%) patients across 7 studies. The majority were 
male patients (55.4%), and the weighted mean age at 
repair was 18.6±12.5 years. Weighted mean follow- up 
duration was 8.6±10.4 years. A total of 594 patients 
across 12 studies had a superior type of SVASD, while 
60 patients in 4 studies had an inferior type of SVASD 
(Table 1).
Operative Data
Various surgical approaches were documented in 
multiple studies, with sternotomy the most common 
technique, employed in a total of 699 (53.0%) patients 
across 18 studies ( Table 1). A subset of studies used 
a mini- sternotomy approach incorporating both upper 
and lower sternal access, which was applied to 55 
(4.2%) patients across 4 studies. A minimally invasive 
approach was adopted in 10 studies, encompassing a 
cohort of 211 (16.0%) patients.
The primary surgical repair most frequently used 
was the single- patch technique across 22 studies in -
volving 676 patients (52.3%) ( Table  1). Alternative re -
pair methods included the 2- patch repair, performed 
on 252 (19.5%) patients across 14 studies, and the 
Warden repair, employed in 8 studies involving 160 
(12.4%) patients. Also, a modified Warden repair was 
reported in 24 (1.9%) patients in a separate study, while 
a double- barrel repair was used in 50 (3.9%) patients 
in yet another study. Other techniques reported in -
cluded repair using a flap of the atrial septum reported 
in a small study (5 patients), as well as primary repair 
through direct suture closure (5 patients across 2 stud-
ies). As for ancillary procedures, secundum ASD repair 
emerged as the most prevalent, with 29 (2.2%) cases 
reported across 3 studies.
Outcomes
There were 3 deaths in the early postoperative pe -
riod, resulting in an in- hospital mortality rate of 0.2%. 
Follow- up data at 30 days were available for 780 pa -
tients across 21 studies, with 4 deaths overall, resulting 
in a 30- day mortality rate of 0.5% (Figure 2). Long- term 
mortality data, including deaths not related to car -
diac or operative causes were available in a few stud-
ies6,15,28,29,45,49 (Table 2). At a weighted mean follow- up 
of 7 days, the in- hospital mortality rate was 0.24% 
(n=3/1242 [95% CI, 0.07%–0.69%]). Descriptive data 
were available for 3 of the 4 deaths in the first 30 days. 
Chu et al report the death of a patient in their 60s with 
diabetes, coronary artery disease, and renal failure, 
who underwent repair of SVASD and partial anoma -
lous pulmonary venous connection. 16 They passed 
away postoperatively after developing bowel ischemia. 
Attenhofer Jost et  al report the death of a patient in 
their 70s with preoperative New York Heart Association 
class IV heart failure who underwent patch closure of

DISCUSSION
SVASD is a rare congenital cardiac anomaly account -
ing for 5% to 10% of all ASDs. 6,59 It results from faulty 
embryological resorption of the sinus venosus at the 
cavo- atrial junction. This typically results in complex 
defects at the confluence of the SVC, right-  sided pul-
monary veins, and superior aspect of the atria. Rarely, 
these defects can involve the inferior vena cava–atrial 
septal confluence. SVASDs are commonly associated 
with other congenital cardiac anomalies, the most 
common being anomalous pulmonary venous return 
involving the right upper pulmonary vein. 6,59
While surgical intervention continues to be the es -
tablished approach for repairing SVASD, transcatheter 
repair is rapidly emerging as an alternative treatment 
strategy and is progressively gaining acceptance. 1–5 
However, a head-  to- head comparison between tran -
scatheter repair and surgery has not been undertaken. 
Moreover, there have not been large multicenter stud-
ies defining the long-  term outcomes of surgical repair 
of SVASD. Consequently, an unmet need exists in es -
tablishing the surgical standard against which novel 
transcatheter therapies can be effectively assessed. 
This systematic review represents the first comprehen-
sive review of the literature on this topic.
Since SVASD is rare, surgical repair is often done in 
specialized centers performing high volumes of con -
genital heart disease surgeries. 6,59 The result is the 
publication of multiple single-  center studies with rel -
atively small cohorts of patients undergoing SVASD 
repair. Several different surgical approaches and tech-
niques have been described. The surgical approaches 
range from standard median sternotomy, anterior and 
posterior thoracotomy, and endoscopic and mini -
mally invasive robotic approaches. 6,8,13,16,42,43,60–62 
Commonly described surgical techniques include 
standard patch repairs, the Warden technique, and 
caval flap repair.6 – 8,10,15,32 While the Warden repair has 
been adopted by many centers as the most common 
repair type, it was reported for ≈15% of patients in the 
literature. This is likely due to multiple factors including 
a long inclusion period for the studies in this review

The landscape of SVASD repairs has undergone 
rapid evolution. Recent publications have brought to 
light the favorable outcomes achieved through min -
imally invasive techniques, and in some cases, even 
robotic- assisted approaches to SVASD repair. 42,60 
Furthermore, as delineated earlier, the adoption of 
transcatheter repair methods has begun to get trac -
tion, although to date there are few studies describing 
the outcomes of transcatheter SVASD repair with het -
erogeneous outcomes. From an anatomic standpoint, 
a substantial proportion, up to 75%, of individuals with 
SVC- type SVASDs could be potential candidates for 
transcatheter repair as the technology and techniques 
advance.4 The key anatomic determinant that influ -
ences the feasibility of transcatheter closure of the in -
teratrial communication and redirection of pulmonary 
venous flow is the direct continuity between the pos -
terior wall of the anomalous pulmonary vein and the 
left atrium. The principal potential advantage of tran -
scatheter closure resides in the brief duration of hos -
pitalization associated with a transcatheter procedure. 
However, patients undergoing transcatheter repair do 
face potential procedural and long-  term risks. These 
risks remain rare but include cardiac perforation, 
hemopericardium, stent embolization, and residual in -
teratrial shunts.4
Considering the exceedingly low mortality risk as -
sociated with both surgical and transcatheter repair of 
SVASD, designing sufficiently powered studies to ef -
fectively compare these treatment approaches poses 
a challenge. Nevertheless, in studies aiming to evalu -
ate these therapeutic strategies, it is imperative to de -
fine appropriate clinical end points. These end points 
should encompass commonly encountered postpro -
cedural complications, such as atrial fibrillation, sinus 
node dysfunction, and pacemaker implantation, as 
well as instances of SVC and pulmonary vein obstruc -
tion. Future studies should aim for clear descriptions 
of surgical approach, repair techniques, and the afore-
mentioned postoperative outcomes to better elucidate 
the outcomes of this patient population and differences 
between the wide range of patients and approaches.
Most reported cohorts of patients who undergo sur-
gical repair have not undergone systematic follow- up 
accompanied by predefined cross-  sectional imaging. 
Consequently, this could lead to an underestimation 
of complications, including the potentially asymptom -
atic instances of SVC and pulmonary vein obstruction, 
particularly when occlusion manifests in an insidious 
manner.

Establishing a standardized framework for document -

surgical approach and repair techniques were hetero -

approaches in their cohort without providing specific 
numbers of each.
CONCLUSIONS
The landscape of surgical management for SVASD has 
undergone rapid evolution, yielding a diverse array of 
surgical approaches and techniques. Multiple centers 
have achieved excellent outcomes, showcasing both 
low operative mortality rates and favorable long-  term 
results. As transcatheter- based methods for SVASD 
repair continue to emerge, they must meet or exceed 
the established surgical benchmarks, especially in 
terms of the long- term risk profile.
