ABSTRACT
The surgical management of patients with complex intracardiac connections, ventricular septal defect (VSD), and
2 adequately sized ventricles is challenging. Understanding the VSD anatomy and its spatial relationships with the
outﬂow tracts is critical to determining the suitability for biventricular repair. Transthoracic 2-dimensional echocardi-
ography (2D-echo) is the primary cardiac imaging tool in congenital heart disease. Transthoracic 3-dimensional
echocardiography (3D-echo) has an additive role over 2D-echo in the de ﬁnition of the potential baf ﬂe pathway from
the VSD to the semilunar valves, the anatomical characteristics of the VSD, and any substrate for systemic out ﬂow tract
obstruction postrepair. In this paper, we describe 4 cases where transthoracic 3D-echo provided crucial delineation
of the intracardiac connections and the VSD anatomy for surgical planning. We also describe our transthoracic
3D-echo protocol to evaluate these complex heart defects. (JACC Case Rep. 2025;30:102755) © 2025 The Authors.

DISCUSSION
Unlike 2D-echo, 3D-echo accurately demonstrates
the VSD shape and size in superior –inferior and
anterior –posterior dimensions. In addition, 3D-echo
identi ﬁes anatomical features leading to potential
intraventricular baf ﬂe obstruction, including
tricuspid valve apparatus traversing the proposed
intraventricular baf ﬂe, prominent infundibular
septum, and long distance from the VSD to the
semilunar valve. We found an excellent correlation
between the 3D-echo ﬁndings, CCT, and surgical
inspection.
Biventricular repair of complex heart defects such
as DORV or congenitally corrected transposition of
the great arteries with VSD involves closure of the
VSD by routing the blood ﬂow from the left
ventricle to the aorta or neoaorta. This procedure
may involve VSD enlargement and resection of
subvalvar out ﬂow tract obstruction.

Transthoracic 3D-echo has technical limitations in
small infants and children because their high heart
rates impact temporal resolution. Imagers should use
the highest-frequency transthoracic 3D-echo trans-
ducer available, narrow the sector width to include
only the region of interest, and optimize the gain
settings to avoid imaging artifacts and improve res-
olution.
4 Small infants or children frequently need to
be sedated to prevent the 3D-echo “stitching ” artifact
related to movement or breathing. Arranging the 3D-
echo with other procedures under anesthesia or even
as part of the preoperative assessment in the oper-
ating room can minimize extra sedation to the pa-
tient. In addition, because of the variable nature of
complex congenital heart disease, the interpretation
of 3D-echo imaging requires expertise to ensure ac-
curacy. It is typical in our center for imagers to
collaborate with each other and involve the surgeons
when interpreting the ﬁndings, particularly when
making surgical decisions.

cardiac connections includes biplane imaging and
full-volume multibeat acquisitions of the heart from
the subxiphoid short-axis and apical views. We use
biplane imaging to visualize the ventricular septum in
2 orthogonal planes and generate an en-face view of
the VSD ( Figures 2A and 2B ) .W ec a nt h e na s s e s st h e
shape and dimensions of the defect in the superior –

process the full-volume multibeat acquisitions to

to visualize the VSD en-face, for shape and location,
