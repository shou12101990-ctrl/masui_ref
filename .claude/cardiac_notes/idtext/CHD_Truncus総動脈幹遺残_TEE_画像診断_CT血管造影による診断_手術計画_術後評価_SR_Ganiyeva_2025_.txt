Abstract 
		  Background: Truncus arteriosus represents a complex congenital heart disease, and accurate diagnostic imaging should be followed 
to provide adequate treatment. Computerized tomography (CT) ang iography has thus been identified as a valuable technique for 
diagnosing, assessing surgical procedures, and tracking post-operative results in patients with these conditions.  
   Methods: The databases PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, plus Google Sc holar were searched for potential studies addressing 
diagnostic accuracy, planning prior to surgery, and follow-up a fter surgery. In this review, analysis was undertaken for data retrieved 
from several CT angiocardiography cases to demonstrate the benefits of the technique and its efficiency.  
   Results: CT angiocardiography is superior  to conventional angiography in  that it is non-invasive and has high-resolution three-
dimensional imaging, which improves the diagnostic ability of truncus arteriosus and the assessment of associated anomalies. It enhances 
preoperative planning since it presents a highly detailed map o f the human body, thus enabling the surgeon to map out the plan ned 
operations and, at the same time, anticipate any complications.  This approach is valuable because postoperative evaluation wit h CT 
angiocardiography is used to determine the success of surgical operations as well as identify residual defects or complications that may 
occur in the future and which would lead to reduced success.  
   Conclusion: Through the use of CT angiocardi ography, managing patients wit h truncus arteriosus has become easier owing to the 
high diagnostic precision provided by the technique, as well as the assistance in proper surgical planning and postoperative monitoring. 
Clinical Practice messages include a call to incorporate CT in Pre-operative & follow-up evaluation.

Introduction 
Truncus arteriosus, or common arterial trunk, is a com-
plex congenital cardiac malformation in which a single ar-
terial duct arising from the heart supplies both the systemic 
and the pulmonary circulation (1). This anomaly is caused 
by the failure of an embryonic truncus arteriosus to divide 
into the aorta and the pulmonary artery during fetal devel-
opment, and thus, it gives only a common arterial trunk that 
overrides a very large ventricular septal defect (VSD).

Truncus arteriosus presents structural and hemodynamic 
problems; therefore, it is actually a combined problem (3). 
The circulatory system configuration is such that the ven-
tricles pump the arterial Trunk, which delivers a combina-
tion of oxygenated and de- oxygenated blood to the sys-
temic circulation, leading to systemic hypoxemia and cya-
nosis (4). In addition, the rise in the pulmonary blood flow 
can cause pulmonary hypertension and increase the risk of 
congestive heart failure if the condition persists (5, 6). 
Truncus arteriosus is referred to as a ‘congenital heart le-
sion’ that commonly manifests in the first few days or 
weeks of infancy; common symptoms include cyanosis, 
respiratory distress, and signs of congestive heart failure. 
These symptoms make it important that first-degree diag-
nostic evaluation is done in effort to start management pro-
tocols (7). 
Early diagnosis of truncus arteriosus for several reasons 
is crucial and requires specific diagnostic techniques (8). 
First, early detection ensures that proper management of 
patients is done through proper medication before they can 
undergo surgery (9). Second, knowledge of the specific 
anatomy of this area is paramount for surgical intervention 
because of dissection patterns and the comorbidities often 
seen in hypoplastic left hearts, including interrupted aortic 
arch or anomalous coronary arteries (10). Medical manage-
ment with balloon dilation angioplasty along with surgical 
intervention, including division of the pulmonary arteries 
from the trunk and creation of a shunt or conduit for the 
passage of the right ventricle and closure of VSD, is done 
ideally in the first few weeks of life to increase the survival 
rate and prognosis (11). 
CT angiocardiography is one of the most effective diag-
nostic tools used in the diagnosis of truncus arteriosus and 
comes with several benefits compared to several diagnostic 
techniques like echocardiography and MRI (12). It affords 
a clear picture of the cardiac structure and any related con-
genital abnormality or distortion that may be seen in the 
defect. Thus, the clear differe nces in the obtained images 
can be helpful in identifying the precise location and size 
of the common arterial trunk, the location of the VSD, and 
the presence of additional vascular pathology (13). 
Rapid image acquisition makes CT angiocardiography 
very useful when utilizing the study in neonates and infants, 
as these patients cannot stand long scanning procedures due 
to potential complications associated with sedation or anes-
thesia (14). Because it is a noninvasive test yet reveals de-
tailed anatomical images, CT angiocardiography can be 
considered part of the first-line investigation for patients 
who are believed to have truncus arteriosus (15). 
While CT angiocardiography indeed helps in the diag-
nostic phase, it also serves to assess the results achieved 
concerning surgical interventions. Outcome assessment by 
CT angiocardiography also helps clinicians assess how well 
the surgical repair has been done, specifically the conduit 
between the right ventricle and the pulmonary arteries (16). 
It also allows for identifying any left-over abnormalities 
that may still be felt within the structures previously left, 
like situations when the VSD is not completely closed, or 
there is stenosis or regurgitation within the repaired area 
(17).  
The main purpose of this review is to explore the essen-
tial function of CT angiocardiography in the diagnosis and 
assessment of the surgical intervention for truncus arterio-
sus. This review seeks to give a mini-review on the benefits 
of CT angiography over other imaging techniques such as 
MRI and PET scans, echo in its ability to provide better de-
tails of the anatomy, the time that it takes to do the scan, 
and its ability to do pre-operative planning as well as post-
operative evaluation. Based on the current literature and 
best practices in clinical care for patients with truncus arte-
riosus, this review aims to highlight the usefulness of CT 
angiocardiography in enhancing patient outcomes.

Role of CT Angiocardiography in Diagnosis 
CT angiocardiography or cardiac CT is a diagnostic im-
aging method that uses no invasive procedure to create il-
lustrations of the cardiac organ and the structures linked 
with it with the aid of CT. In the case of diagnosing truncus 
arteriosus, the purpose of goal orientation is to provide 
high-quality images that are detailed with respect to the 
shape and positioning of the heart and significant blood 
vessels (22). The standard protocol for CT angiocardiog-
raphy involves several steps (23, 24): 
Preparing the patient well for the test may involve an 
overnight fast and the use of beta-blocker drugs, which help 
reduce the rate of heartbeat and hence enhance image clar-
ity (25). Contrast media is given intravenously to better vis-
ualize the cardiac chambers, arteries, and veins. There is 
primarily a species difference in the timing of contrast in-
jection and secondarily in the sequence for getting the best 
opacification of the cardiovascular structures (26). Dual-
source CT scanners provide images in multiple phases, but 
the phase most often needed is the arterial phase, which 
gives images of systemic and pulmonary arteries. Current 
state-of-the-art multi-detector Computed Tomography

Methods 
General Background 
Based on the identified articles, this review will be aimed 
at emphasizing the use of CT angiocardiography in the di-
agnosis, preoperative planning as well as postoperative as-
sessment of truncus arteriosus. It is, therefore, the intention 
to review and integrate recen t literature and clinical ap-
proaches that best explain the importance and utility of CT 
angiocardiography in targeting this complex CHD (27).  
 
Inclusion Criteria 
To ensure a comprehensive and relevant review, the fol-
lowing inclusion criteria were applied to select studies and 
articles:  
Study Type:  Only articles published in peer-reviewed 
journals and only primary research on species, clinical tri-
als, systematic review, and meta-analysis were considered. 
Language: The articles were published in the English 
language in the international database. 
Publication Date: Research papers that have been made 
available within the last two decades to make sure that the 
findings are up to date with the latest developments pro-
pelled by advanced technologies. 
Population: Research was conducted on neonatal, in-
fancy, and childhood subjects with confirmed diagnoses of 
truncus arteriosus. 
Intervention: Articles pertaining to the applications of 
CT angiocardiography for the diagnosis, preoperative lo-
calization, and postoperative assessment of patients with 
truncus arteriosus. 
Outcomes: Original articles assess the diagnostic perfor-
mance of CT angiocardiography, evaluate surgical strategy 
and feasibility, conduct post-su rgical analysis, and cite 
long-term data. 
 
Exclusion Criteria 
The following exclusion criteria were used to filter out 
irrelevant studies: 
Study Type: Systematic reviews and meta-analyses, clin-
ical trials, and policies do not meet these criteria; excluded 
studies also included any that  were conference abstracts, 
editorials, or opinions written in a letter or with letters be-
tween the authors. 
Language: Some of the articles have not been published 
in English. 
Publication Date: Research work that is over two dec-
ades, barring research studies that are referred to as classic 
papers that give basic ideas. 
Population: Research based on adult participants or 
other defects of congenital heart diseases, excluding trun-
cus arteriosus. 
Intervention: Previously published papers or studies that 
explain any other imaging modality in detail without com-
parison to CT angiocardiography or articles that do not ad-
dress CT angiocardiography at all. 
Outcomes: Any work for which there is no distinct result 
concerning the diagnostic capability assessment, surgical 
planning, or post-surgery assessment. 
 
 
Information Sources 
The literature review involved searching multiple elec-
tronic databases to identify relevant studies and articles:  
PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane.

Data Collection 
The data collection process included:  
Search Strategy: Specific s earch with conceivable tech-
nical terms and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH terms) 
of ‘truncus arteriosus’, ‘CT angiocardiography’, ‘diagno-
sis’, ‘pre-operative planning’, and post-operative evalua-
tion. Screening: Titles and abstracts were screened to judge 
the relevance of articles. In the next step, studies retrieved 
through the search were screened for their relevance by ref-
erencing the objective criteria for inclusion and exclusion. 
Data Extraction:  The information from the included 
study was collected using the extraction form, where some 
of the details included general information about the study, 
trial details, patient information, CT angiocardiography 
procedural details, and results. 
 
Synthesis of Evidence  
In this case, the study encompasses a narrative review 
since it involves the integration and analysis of qualitative 
data and descriptive statistics. Where possible, the sensitiv-
ity, specificity, and diagnostic accuracy of each study were 
mentioned, including the percentage rates as well.  
 
Results 
A systematic search employing a specific search strategy 
yielded 800 articles initially. Subsequent screening based 
on titles and abstracts narrowed down the selection to 150 
articles for full-text evaluation. Upon thorough assessment, 
56 articles were deemed relevant and subsequently utilized 
to inform and construct this review (Figure 1). 
The findings of several studies also show how angiocar-
diography with CT is more effective than other diagnostic 
techniques in obtaining high-resolution imaging in a three-
dimensional format required for the correct diagnosis and 
for follow-up, both pre and post-surgical. 
A recent technique known as CT angiocardiography has 
been useful in diagnosing truncus arteriosus and other rele-
vant abnormalities (37, 38). This spatial resolution helps to 
provide a clear definition of the common arterial trunk, 
ventricular septal defect (VSD), and other vascular lesions, 
including the aortic arch and coronary arteries (39, 40).  
The literature review has revealed that numerous authors 
state that the diagnostic yield of CT angiocardiography is 
higher compared to other imaging techniques, such as those 
employed by echocardiography or MRI, due to improved 
ability to depict both the general vascular anatomy and spe-
cific, minor details (41, 42). The pre-surgical visualization 
of the detailed anatomy by CT angiocardiography is much 
easier than in conventional angiography (43). The anatom-
ical correlations and operative models can present surgeons 
with a clear perspective of the positional orientation be-
tween different portions of the heart. It has been observed 
that this endoscopic view greatly aids in visualizing the ex-
act location and size of the VSD, the course of coronary 
arteries, and the shape and si ze of pulmonary arteries. For

offering high-quality images, which are vital in surgical

CHD and are prone to be seen during surgical interventions. 
Therefore, the utilization of dual-source CT angiography as 
a beneficial tool for guide-line CHD assessment and pre-
operative planning improves surgical results and patient

Discussion 
This review discusses the use of CT angiocardiography 
in the diagnosis and assessment after surgery for children 
with truncus arteriosus, a congenital heart disease. Our dis-
cussion ties the existing literature and current practices to 
evidence the usefulness of CT angiocardiography for man-
aging truncus arteriosus. Compared with other imaging di-
agnostic tools such as echocardiography and MRI, CT an-
giocardiography offers an accu rate anatomical depiction 
for diagnosis. It provides good resolution enhanced three-
dimensional imaging for the common arterial trunk, ven-
tricular septal defect, and associated abnormalities such as 
aortic arch and coronary artery anomalies. This level of de-
tail is essential in helping the clinician make a correct diag-
nosis and in trying to further evaluate the severity of the 
defect. 
This article brings out the importance of CT angiocardi-
ography, especially in planning for surgery. It is particu-
larly useful in defining the su rgical strategy because it de-
tects other associated congenital anomalies like pulmonary 
artery and coronary artery. Three-dimensional reconstruc-
tions, along with surgical mapping, help in detailed plan-
ning and thus provide the surgeon with a map and view of 
the field of operation and potential problems ahead. De-
scriptions of patient encounters show that CT angiocardi-
ography has changed surgical plans, making them more 
specific and appropriate.  
CT angiocardiography is also of paramount importance 
in the follow-up assessment since it offers a platform for 
evaluating the success of surgical interventions. It can as-
sess the residual defects, such as the incompletely closed 
VSD or problems with the conduit, and timely identify 
complications, such as stenosis or regurgitation after the 
operation. Additionally, CT angiocardiography is used for 
the follow-up of patients as this technique provides detailed 
images of the anatomy after surgical corrections to deter-
mine durability and early signs of complications requiring 
reintervention (53).

There are different modalities to diagnose congenital 
heart diseases, such as truncus arteriosus. CT angiocardiog-
raphy is a superior technique in terms of anatomical detail 
and is invaluable for preoperative planning and assessment 
of the intricate anatomy of CHDs. Its high frame rate also 
means that patients do not have to be kept under heavy se-
dation for long durations, a unique attribute that endears it 
to the children admitted in  critical conditions, notwith-
standing the fact that most modalities entail exposure to ra-
diation. Echocardiography is more commonly used first 
through imaging studies because it does not require the in-
troduction of a contrast agent and provides a real-time view 
(54).  
However, its potential is somewhat less when one re-
quires detailed information about the organs’ anatomy or 
when acoustic windows are compromised. MRI creates 
clear images without the use of radiation and is ideal for 
imaging the body structures and their functions. However, 
its application is somewhat limited. More time is taken in 
acquisition, and the patient may have to cooperate or be 
placed under some anesthesia. T his technique, therefore, 
despite being invasive, remains the gold standard in the as-
sessment of patients’ hemodynamics and the provision of 
interventional procedures. This is most commonly applied 
in those situations in which other imaging modalities are 
incapable of supplying adequate detail (55) (Table 1). 
Heterotopic ventricles are commonly found in associa-
tion with complex CHD, and there are other associated vas-
cular lesions which will alter the surgical strategy. Some of 
these variations are obviously surgical targets that should 
be understood well before undertaking any intervention; 
another example is variation in the aortic arch. Although 
routine 2D echocardiograms are helpful, they might have 
some deficiencies in assessing the branching patterns of the 
aortic arch. As such, it is recommended that CT angi-
ography using low doses should be performed to overcome 
these limitations. The advantage of using CT angiography 
is the ability to obtain high-resolution three-dimensional 
images that may portray anatomical details and deviations 
that are improbable to be identified using echocardiography 
(56, 57).  
This technique offers a unique opportunity to visualize 
the vascular system in detail and helps in the effective plan-
ning of operations before surgery is performed. With the 
help of volume rendering images and 3D models gained 
from CT angiography data, a better understanding of such 
complex vascular anomalies can be provided, which will 
help surgeons preview possible complications during sur-
gery and improve surgical strategies. Therefore, the appli-
cation of low-dose CT angiography as a part of serial ex-
aminations of patients with complex congenital heart dis-
ease can significantly contribute to more effective planning

Conclusion 
Currently, CT angiocardiography plays a significant role 
in acquiring three-dimensional imaging in three planes, 
which is vital for adequate diagnosis and detection of other 
congenital disorders in patients with truncus arteriosus. In-
troduced technical refinements in CT angiocardiography 
bring improvements in imaging definition at the tissue level 
and, therefore, lead to better mapping and depiction of the 
anatomy of the common arterial trunk, ventricular septal 
defect, and other associated vascular anomalies.  
There is nothing like too much information in the diag-
nosis of this type of problem; it helps in developing appro-
priate strategies for managing the congenital heart defect. 
Apart from the diagnostic utility, CTA serves a great role 
in pre-surgery planning accurate ly. In addition to detailed 
3D images and surgical copyright, CT angiocardiography 
also provides visualization of the spatial topography be-
tween different structures in the heart. Detailed stream 
mapping helps in determining the exact anatomic location 
and size of the VSD, its shape and size, the course of the 
coronary arteries, and the morphology of the pulmonary ar-
teries.  
As such, it allows for better postoperative planning of 
surgeons’ operations and minimizes the risk of intraopera-
tive complications ,improving the likelihood of favorable 
surgical outcomes. In addition, CT Angiocardiography’s 
main use is in the assessment and follow-up after the recov-
ery from surgery. It enables comprehensive evaluation of 
the surgical reconstruction, including patency and function-
ality of RV outflow conduit to t he pulmonary arteries, as 
well as the function of VSD septal sutures.  
Furthermore, CT angiocardiography can visualize resid-
ual abnormalities or arterial switch operation (ASO)-re-
lated early complications, including the areas of stenosis or 
regurgitation, which is vital for appropriate management. 
By performing follow-up with the CT angiocardiography, 
close surveillance is effective in detecting any problems 
with the repaired structures, thus enhancing the durability 
and effectiveness of treatment for the patients. 
 
Recommendations and Future Research 
Our review suggests that CT angiocardiography should 
be adopted as standard imaging in pre-surgical planning for 
patients with truncus arteriosus owing to its high anatomi-
cal resolution. Follow-up protocols using CT need to be im-
plemented to assess surgical outcomes and to identify com-
plications early. Carefully combined interpretation by car-
diologists, radiologists, and surgeons significantly contrib-
utes to clear communication a nd effective surgical deci-
sion-making. Further, reducing radiation dose through 
technology improvement and increasing the educational 
level of clinicians in the CT application are also important. 
Future research will need to include long-term follow-up 
studies to assess patient outcomes, investigation of new and 
advanced modalities of CT to increase its specificity while 
minimizing radiation exposure, and comparison of CT with 
other imaging modalities. Establishing CT-specific imag-
ing protocols for children and randomized controlled trials 
to measure the effects of CT-facilitated procedures on sur-
gical outcomes also have potential. Improving these areas 
will make managing truncus arteriosus through CT angio-
cardiography more clinically useful and provide better pa-
tient care.
