Abstract 
Objectives: Despite the increasing use of the en bloc rotation of the outflow tracts to treat patients with transposition of 
the great arteries with left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, long-term outcome data are scarce. Cases with simultan -
eous Mustard operation are a challenging technical expansion. This retrospective study aims to evaluate long-term out -
comes in a single-centre cohort.

Methods: All patients undergoing the en bloc rotation at the Children’s Heart Center Linz between 2003 and 2024 were 
retrospectively analysed.
Results: The en bloc rotation was performed in 38 patients. Median age at operation was 4.1 (0.7; 23.9) months, weight 
5.4 (3.7; 9.6) kg. A congenitally corrected transposition was present in 3 patients, in which a simultaneous Mustard proced -
ure was performed. Median cardiopulmonary bypass time was 291 (270; 353) minutes, aortic cross-clamp time 160 (142; 
185) minutes. The pulmonary valve could be preserved in 24 children (63%). Two patients died during the hospital stay 
(5%). During a follow-up period of median 9.3 (4.4; 14.3) years, 3 further children deceased, 5 surgical reinterventions 
were necessary. There was no death or surgical reintervention in the congenitally corrected transposition group.
Conclusions: The long-term outcome of the en bloc rotation is very promising, as could be expected due to the anatomic 
repair with growth potential in all tubular structures. The increased experience also with the Mustard operation allowed 
the combination of both complex procedures with excellent short-term outcome.

Introduction
The transposition of the great arteries (TGA) and similar 
cases of double outlet right ventricle (DORV) as well as 
the congenitally corrected TGA (ccTGA) with ventricular 
septal defect (VSD) and left ventricular outflow tract ob -
struction (LVOTO) are rare and complex congenital heart 
defects.
1
Several centres have adopted the en bloc rota -
tion of the outflow tracts (EBR)/half turned truncal switch 
operation as an anatomic repair with growth potential in 
all cardiac structures over the last years.
2-8
Data on long- 
term outcomes are rarely available, and cases with simul -
taneous Mustard operation are a challenging technical 
expansion. The aim of this retrospective single-centre 
study was to evaluate mortality and reintervention rates 
during a follow-up period of up to 21.7 years.
Patients and methods
This retrospective study was approved by the ethics com -
mittee of the Medical Faculty at the Johannes Kepler 
University Linz on 12 February 2025 (EK Nr: 1011/2025). 
Informed consent was waived because of the retrospect -
ive nature of the study and the use of pseudonymized 
clinical data for the analysis.
All patients who underwent the EBR at the Children’s 
Heart Center Linz between 2003 and 2024 were included 
in this study. Primary end-points were mortality and rein -
terventions. Secondary end-points included assessments 
of valve and ventricular function, as well as postoperative 
complications such as extracorporeal membrane oxygen -
ation (ECMO) support, bleeding requiring re-thoracotomy, 
AV-block necessitating pacemaker implantation, renal fail -
ure requiring peritoneal dialysis (KDIGO stage 3) and pro -
longed chylothorax requiring drainage. Pre-, peri-, and 
postoperative data were obtained from our institutional 
databases. The last follow-up was defined as the latest 
clinical examination including transthoracic echocardiog -
raphy, which was routinely carried out at our hospital or 
by a paediatric cardiologist cooperating closely with our 
institution. One patient was lost to follow-up. In this case, 
values from the last ultrasound examination before 
discharge were taken into account. Results from postoper -
ative follow-up investigations couldn’t be reliably inter -
preted in 3 additional patients. The inclusion of their 
follow-up data was therefore limited to the primary end- 
points. Postoperative pulmonary valve diameters could 
not be obtained by transthoracic echocardiography; there -
fore, flow velocity was used as an alternative parameter to 
characterize pulmonary valve function.
Operative technique
In the preoperative planning, a transthoracic echocardi -
ography was carried out routinely. An additional angiog -
raphy was only performed if there were residual 
uncertainties; a CT scan was used in 2 cases of ccTGA pre -
operatively. The approach for all 38 surgeries was a me -
dian sternotomy, and cold crystalloid cardioplegia was 
used. The technique for the EBR did not change signifi -
cantly over the years and was described earlier in 
detail.
3 , 9 , 10
In the 3 cases of ccTGA with VSD and pulmon -
ary stenosis (detailed anatomy see Table 1 ), the Mustard 
procedure was performed first and then the aortic cross- 
clamp was opened. The heart was re-perfused for at least 
a third of this first cross-clamp time. During a second 
clamping time, the EBR was performed. The material for 
the Mustard baffle was fresh autologous pericardium, for 
the VSD patch and pulmonary artery reconstruction, the 
rest of the autologous pericardium was fixated by gluta -
raldehyde. The pulmonary bifurcation was transferred a 
few millimetres towards the right pulmonary artery in 
one patient and towards the left pulmonary artery in an -
other patient.
Statistics
Time-to-event variables were depicted by Kaplan-Meier 
plots. Kaplan-Meier estimates were supplemented by 
2-sided confidence bands (pointwise approach). The 
probability of reintervention was estimated by using the 
Aalen-Johansen method, treating death as a competing

event. For event rates, 2-sided 95% CI were calculated 
according to Clopper-Pearson. The influence of the 
chronological order of operations on the occurrence of 
surgical or transcatheter reintervention was investigated 
by Cox regression. The type I error was not adjusted for 
multiple testing. Therefore, the results of inferential sta -
tistics are descriptive only. Statistical analyses were per -
formed using the open-source R statistical software 
package, version 4.5.1 (The R Foundation for Statistical 
Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables are 
reported as medians and quartiles (in square brackets) 
and range (minimum and maximum in brackets); and cat -
egorical variables are expressed as counts and percen -
tages, with CI in brackets.
Results
Between 2003 and 2024, in total, 38 patients have under -
gone the EBR at the Children’s Heart Center Linz. The 
patients’ characteristics are provided in Table 1 . Seven 
patients had 1-5 palliative steps previously, 2 of them at 
our centre. Due to the increased experience with the EBR 
as well as with the Mustard operation at our centre the 
first combination of both procedures for the repair of a 
ccTGA with LVOTO was performed in the year 2023. Two 
further Mustard þ EBR cases followed; the age at oper -
ation was 1, 5.5, and 7 years, respectively. One of these 
children (SLL) had already needed a pacemaker implant -
ation because of an intermittent AV block III 3 years prior 
to the anatomic correction.
Postoperative data and early mortality
Peri- and postoperative data are provided in Table 2 . 
Thirty-six patients (95%) could be discharged from the 
hospital, 2 patients (5%) died during their stay in the in -
tensive care unit (ICU). These were the only cases that 
required postoperative ECMO: Patient 14 was a severely 
cyanotic 3-year-old girl with venovenous fistula status 
post bidirectional Glenn operation. After EBR, she needed 
ECMO twice and could be weaned. Nevertheless, she 
died after a case of pneumonia on postoperative day 
(POD) 57. Patient 33 was duct dependent due to a severe 
subvalvular pulmonary stenosis, had the complex situ -
ation of a situs inversus totalis and was operated on her 
7th day of life. The operation was very complicated with 
an aortic cross-clamp time of in total 192 minutes and a 
bypass time of 520 minutes. After a primarily successful 
weaning off bypass, she was resuscitated in the ICU a 
few hours later and received ECMO therapy for 8 days. 
Furthermore, she required peritoneal dialysis due to se -
vere renal failure and a pacemaker implantation because 
of an AV-block III. Unfortunately, she died due to multior -
gan failure on POD 31.
A pacemaker implantation due to postoperative AV- 
block II or III was necessary in 4 more patients. Three of 
them were out of the TGA group; they were provided 
with the EBR as newborns.

The fourth pacemaker was 
implanted in a boy with a ccTGA, the AV-block had al -
ready developed after completion of the Mustard compo -
nent of the procedure.

The most relevant results are presented in Table 3 . 
During a median follow-up period of 9.3 (4.4; 14.3) years, 
33 patients are alive (1.6 deaths per 100 patient-years).

98%) and 85% (62-97%), respectively. Three patients died 
late, all of them within the first year postoperatively: One

Discussion
The EBR is an anatomic correction of the very complex 
TGA or DORV/TGA with VSD and left ventricular outflow 
tract obstruction. The main advantage over the alterna -
tive Rastelli procedure is the straight outflow tracts 
without a long intracardiac baffle.
4
In contrast to the 
Nikaidoh procedure, the coronary arteries always have to 
be excised and reimplanted during the EBR. However, all 
alternative techniques including the REV procedure re -
quire a reconstruction of the right ventricular outflow 
tract without the potential of keeping the autologous pul -
monary valve.
12 , 13
By performing an EBR even in the case 
of a too small pulmonary valve at least parts of native 
structures of the right ventricular outflow tract are pre -
served, which provides the growth potential of all tubular 
structures
4 , 10
( Figure 3A and B ).
Nevertheless, the EBR is a highly complex procedure, 
and the justification of performing this operation 
demands a review of the early postoperative and espe -
cially the long-term outcome regularly. More and more 
centres have adopted the technique over the last few 
years, and few studies have been published. Especially in 
patients where the pulmonary valve is too small and a 
transannular patch is required, the advantage of the EBR 
compared to the Nikaidoh procedure needs to be careful -
ly discussed. In our opinion, the Nikaidoh procedure is 
also a technically demanding operation, and the intro -
duction of 2 different complex procedures for a rare 
underlying heart defect at a single centre should be con -
sidered with caution. Stoica et al
1
performed a multi -
centre study including 27 of our patients recently and 
found a slight advantage of the EBR in comparison to the 
Nikaidoh procedure mainly due to the low reintervention 
rates on the right ventricular outflow tract. In another 
previous study published by our centre the postoperative 
growth of the pulmonary valves could be shown.
3
These 
findings can be supported by our present results, as 
there still has not been any surgical reintervention on the 
pulmonary valves or transannular patches 5 years and 11 
additional patients later. Weixler et al
5
compared the ar -
terial switch operation þ left ventricular outflow tract 
obstruction-resection, half-turned truncal switch/Mair 
(¼EBR), Nikaidoh and Rastelli at their single centre with 
very good outcomes. In their study, reinterventions on 
the right ventricular outflow tract were frequent, with 
the highest rates after Nikaidoh and Rastelli procedures.

Regarding the reintervention rate within our cohort, 
we saw that transcatheter reinterventions were mainly 
performed at the beginning of our series. The only excep -
tion was the stent for the Mustard baffle in one of our lat -
est patients. Surgical reinterventions because of a 
residual VSD or patch plasties of the pulmonary arteries 
were also limited to the early cases. A Hazard ratio <1 
showed that the risk for a surgical or transcatheter rein -
tervention was lower in the later cases of our series. This 
indicates a learning curve, although not statistically sig -
nificant (P ¼ .178). However, in total, 2 patients with aortic 
insufficiency requiring surgery 4.1 and 3.7 years after the 
EBR (patient 5 and patient 29) require discussion. Three 
more children showed aortic regurgitation more than 
mild at their last follow-up echocardiography 15, 14, and 
8 years after EBR. In none of these patients, a pathology 
of the aortic valve more than mild observed at their hos -
pital discharge. A Kaplan-Meier curve describing the free -
dom from aortic regurgitation (more than mild) and 
aortic valve reintervention is provided as Figure S1 . The 
Berlin group did not see an indication for an aortic valve 
surgery in their half-turned truncal switch/EBR cohort 
(n ¼ 23) after a median follow-up of 0.7 years (IQR 0.1-4.5 
years). They found one patient (4.3%) with an aortic re -
gurgitation more than moderate at the last follow-up. 
The same finding was described in 2 cases out of their ar -
terial switch cohort (10%) and in 4 patients after Rastelli 
procedure (9.3%). There was no statistical difference be -
tween groups (P ¼ .5).
5
Hongu et al
4
describe one patient 
with moderate aortic regurgitation requiring valvulo -
plasty 8 years postoperatively. However, this was a case 
with a bicuspid aortic valve. Liu et al
12
reported 174 
patients after double-root translocation; they only found 
2/150 cases (1.3%) of aortic regurgitation mild or greater 
after a median follow-up time of 7.8 years. They propose 
that the aortic root should be re-implanted by interrupted 
stitches to prevent annulus dilatation. At our centre, we 
have been using the continuous suture technique in neo -
natal Ross-Konno procedures as well and did not find any 
neoaortic insufficiency postoperatively.
14
Looking at the 
total 5 patients with aortic regurgitation more than mild (2 
repair cases included), we did not see an influence of the 
underlying anatomy (DORV/TGA vs TGA), gender or surgi -
cal era. The only finding was the age at surgery: these 5 
patients were provided with the EBR during their first year 
of life, but not as a newborn (age at operation 40 to 365 
days). Late aortic insufficiency has also been reported fol -
lowing Bex-Nikaidoh procedures.
8
Some centres have 
noted a potential association between aortic regurgitation 
and coronary artery detachment and reimplantation.
15
However, as both cases of aortic regurgitation out of our 
EBR series could be repaired by a subvalvular annulo -
plasty, the problem seems to be the VSD size, shape and 
implantation technique rather than other technical issues 
as the coronary artery implantation.
11
A concordant con -
clusion was published by Schulz et al.
8
The previously reported in-hospital mortality has been 
addressed in earlier publications, and no additional fatal 
events have occurred since. There were no signs of 
coronary-related complications postoperatively.
3 , 10
The 3 
late mortality cases happened during the first postopera -
tive year and were not directly procedure-related (see 
“Results”). As the most recent echocardiography demon -
strated preserved ventricular function in all patients ex -
cept one (see “Results”), no evidence indicative of coronary 
issues was identified. Including 16 patients with a follow- 
up period >10 years, the expectations for the EBR to be a 
sustainable therapy with low late mortality stay high.
The series now includes 3 patients with ccTGA. The 
combination of an atrial switch procedure with the EBR 
or with a double root translocation had already been per -
formed successfully and published by the colleagues 
from Berlin and Fuwai Hospital Beijing.
5 , 12
Two of our 
cases were completely uneventful with an in-hospital stay 
of 14 days. The third patient with SLL transposition 
required a pacemaker implantation 13 days postopera -
tively because of an AV block III, which already appeared 
after the Mustard part of the surgery. Due to the above- 
described chylothorax, he was discharged from the hos -
pital on POD 52. All 3 patients were doing really well at 
their last follow-up examinations.
The limitations of this study are its retrospective single- 
centre design, the small patient cohort and the short 
follow-up period of the ccTGA cases. Further multicentre 
studies will be necessary and useful.
Conclusion
The long-term outcome of the EBR is very promising, as 
could be expected due to the anatomic repair with 
straight outflow tracts and growth potential in all tubular 
cardiac structures. No reintervention on the right ven -
tricular outflow tract or pulmonary valve was necessary 
until now. Two cases with aortic insufficiency requiring 
Cosgrove plasty demand discussion of the VSD patch im -
plantation technique. The increased experience with the 
EBR and with the Mustard operation at our centre 
allowed the combination of both complex procedures 
with excellent short-term outcome.
