Decades of single-center experience in managing 274 
double-outlet right ventricle patients: Biventricular repair 
suitable for more complex forms?

ABSTRACT
Objective: To investigate the long-term outcomes of double-outlet right ventricle 
(DORV), focusing on identifying specific subtypes capable of achieving successful 
biventricular repair (BVR) with favorable results.
Methods: A retrospective review was conducted of 274 DORV patients followed at 
a single center from 1999 to 2023. After exclusions, 238 patients were analyzed, 
including 97 who underwent single ventricle palliation (SVP) and 141 who under-
went BVR. Outcomes were assessed using Kaplan-Meier survival and freedom 
from reintervention estimates, with competing risk analysis for survival, transplan-
tation, or death.
Results: The median follow-up was 11.9 years. Subaortic and subpulmonary ventric-
ular septal defects (VSDs) were more common in the BVR group, while noncom-
mitted VSDs and features suggestive of heterotaxy (eg, persistent left superior 
vena cava, complete atrioventricular septal defect) were more frequent in SVP. 
BVR tended toward improved transplantation-free survival (8% vs 16% for trans-
plantation or death; P = .06). At 40 years post-repair, estimated outcomes were 
70% survival, 8% transplantation, and 20% mortality for BVR, compared to 
45%, 18%, and 37%, respectively, for SVP. Subgroup analysis showed equivalent 
survival in noncommitted, subpulmonary, and doubly committed types, with supe-
rior survival in subaortic DORV after BVR (100% vs 75%; P = .025). Freedom from 
reintervention was also comparable across subtypes.
Conclusions: Our study shows that BVR was associated with improved outcomes 
compared to SVP, including higher survival rates and lower rates of both mortality 
and transplantation,. Decades of experience at our institution demonstrates the 
feasibility of BVR in complex DORV subtypes, such as noncommitted and subpul-
monary VSD. (JTCVS Open 2026;30:101694)

PERSPECTIVE
Our study found better survival, lower mortality, 
and reduced rates of transplantation with biven-
tricular repair compared to single ventricle 
repair, reaffirming its efficacy in challenging 
cases.
Double-outlet right ventricle (DORV) encompasses a het-
erogeneous group of congenital heart anomalies character-
ized by at least 50% of the great arteries originating from 
the morphologic right ventricle, along with variations in 
the relationship between the great arteries and the ventricu-
lar septal defect (VSD). 1 The original definition, introduced 
by Lev and colleagues 2 in 1972, has since been refined by
the STS Congenital Heart Surgery Nomenclature and Data-
base Committee. 1 According to the updated definition, 
DORV represents a type of ventriculoarterial connection 
in which both great vessels arise either entirely or predom-
inantly from the right ventricle. 1 For the purposes of this 
study, we adhere to the earlier definition, which categorizes 
DORV into 4 subtypes based on the location of the VSD:

Among these subtypes, subaortic VSD is the most com-
mon, while ncVSD poses the greatest surgical challenge 
owing to the remote positioning of the VSD relative to the

staged biventricular repair (BVR) or single ventricle pallia-

by patient-specific and anatomic factors. Of note, an 
increasing number of patients with complex forms of 
DORV are being considered for BVR 3,4 ; however, previous 
studies have highlighted the challenges associated with 
complex BVR. For instance, Li and colleagues 4 reported 
lower overall survival rates at 6 months, 1 year, and 5 years 
for ncVSD compared to other VSD subtypes.
Despite these challenges, questions remain regarding the 
long-term viability of primary or staged BVR for more 
complex lesions. Bradley and colleagues 5 demonstrated 
that BVR was associated with higher late mortality and re-
intervention rates compared to SVP, the latter being espe-
cially important with regard to health-related quality of 
life. In contrast, other studies, such as those by Lacour-
Gayet and colleagues 3 and Oladunjoye and colleagues, 6 
suggest that favorable outcomes can be achieved with 
BVR, even in complex cases.
In this study, using the longest follow-up reported for a 
cohort of DORV patients, we aimed to determine the preva-
lence of BVR and SVP and evaluate the long-term outcomes 
of DORV, with a specific focus on identifying subtypes 
amenable to successful BVR with favorable results.
MATERIALS AND METHODS
Study Design and Patients
This is a single-center, retrospective review of all patients 
diagnosed with DORV who either underwent surgical 
intervention or were followed at Cleveland Clinic Hospi-
tal(s) between 1999 and 2023. Institutional Review Board 
approval (IRB 24-092) was obtained on January 29, 2024, 
classifying the study as exempt human subjects research. 
Consent was not required.
Diagnosis of DORV was confirmed in 274 patients based 
on echocardiographic findings, surgical reports, and
independent reviews conducted by multiple investigators 
(JR, AA, PC, and TK). The diagnostic criteria adhered to 
the “50% rule,” 7 and the subclassifications followed the 
system proposed by Lev and colleagues. 2
Demographic, diagnostic, surgical, and outcome data 
were extracted from hospital records using the electronic 
health record system. Clinical status at the most recent 
follow-up, defined as a phone contact or clinic visit docu-
mented in the electronic heart record, was recorded.
Statistical Analysis
Data are presented as frequency, median with interquar-
tile range, or mean ± standard deviation, as appropriate. 
Categorical variables are summarized as count and percent-
age and were compared using the χ 2 test or Fisher exact test, 
as indicated. Time-dependent events were analyzed using 
the Kaplan-Meier method. Competing risks analyses were 
conducted following previously reported methodologies. 8,9 
All statistical analyses were performed using R version 
4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). 
Two-tailed tests were used, with a P value<.05 considered 
statistically significant.

BVR in Challenging Anatomic Subtypes
Historically, certain DORV subtypes—particularly 
ncVSD and subpulmonic VSD (Taussig-Bing)—have 
been considered high-risk candidates for BVR owing to 
the technical complexity of the intracardiac rerouting 
required. For instance, in cases of ncVSD, significant
straddling of the tricuspid valve, particularly when accom-
panied by ventricular hypoplasia, may preclude the feasi-
bility of BVR. However, when ventricular development is 
adequate, BVR remains a viable option, with viable surgical 
strategies to achieve repair. 10-12 However, the surgeon needs 
an in-depth understanding of the atrioventricular conduc-
tion system anatomy to avoid surgically induced heart 
block, especially when enlarging the VSD anterosuperiorly, 
as the conduction pathways in these cases often deviate 
from the typical arrangement. 12,13
DORV with subpulmonic VSD is frequently associated 
with concomitant cardiac anomalies, particularly aortic 
arch anomalies and RVOT obstruction, which can compli-
cate surgical management. Several surgical techniques to 
optimize outcomes for these patients have been described. 
One approach involves rerouting the VSD to the aorta using 
a long-intracardiac baffle; however, this carries the risk of 
several complications, including tricuspid and pulmonary 
valve impairment and left ventricular outflow tract

BVR in DORV with Subaortic VSD
Our results confirm that among DORV subtypes, sub-
aortic VSD is the most favorable candidate for BVR, as 
anticipated and has been reported by others. 3,5,6 Although 
most patients with subaortic VSD were suitable for BVR, 
a subset in our cohort underwent SVP. Patients selected 
for SVP typically exhibited hypoplasia of the left and/or 
right ventricle in conjunction with additional anatomic 
complexities, such as mitral valve atresia, pulmonary 
atresia, diminutive pulmonary arteries, or a straddling 
tricuspid valve. Nevertheless, our results align with existing 
literature emphasizing subaortic VSD as the simplest 
anatomic DORV subtype to repair owing to its favorable 
VSD–great artery alignment, reducing the need for complex 
intracardiac tunneling. 24
BVR Does Not Increase the Reintervention Rate
Although BVR is associated with better survival, lower 
transplantation rates, and nearly equivalent reintervention 
rates compared to SVP, it does not obviate the need for 
future reintervention. As shown in Table 2, overall 64 of 
238 patients needed at least 1 reintervention, with compara-
ble proportions of reintervention in the BVR (48 of 141) and 
SVP (16 of 97) groups. Although the difference is not statis-
tically significant, it holds clinical significance, with 
notable variations in the nature of reinterventions. Patients 
undergoing BVR were more likely to require RVOT reinter-
ventions, which may reflect the technical demands of intra-
cardiac tunneling procedures that can reduce the effective 
RV volumes, simply the need for RV–pulmonary artery 
conduit upsizing or replacement, or the need for pulmonary 
valve replacement in patients receiving a transannular 
patch. In contrast, SVP patients largely needed collateral 
occlusions, tricuspid valve repairs, and SVP fenestration 
closure, emphasizing the long-term hemodynamic burden 
of SVP and reinforcing concerns about progressive SVP 
failure over time. 25-28
No significant differences between the BVR and SVP 
groups were observed with regard to freedom from reinter-
vention at 14 years for ncVSD and subpulmonic VSD sub-
types. This suggests that BVR does not necessarily reduce 
the need for later reinterventions in complex DORV vari-
ants, potentially due to residual VSD, conduit obstruction, 
or pulmonary valve dysfunction. 29,30 These findings empha-
size that while BVR may improve survival, its impact on 
morbidity in these subtypes remains undefined. Future 
research should examine whether modifications to BVR 
strategies can improve long-term durability in these patients.
SVP outcome studies have identified several complica-
tions, including progressive circulatory failure, ventricular 
dysfunction, arrhythmias, and protein-losing enteropathy, 
along with an increased rate of heart transplantation. 31-33 
Our findings emphasize the life-long implications of the 
initial surgical decision, reinforcing the importance of BVR 
whenever technically feasible to avoid SVP-related 
complications.
Our findings indicate that heterotaxy-related anomalies 
such as persistent left superior vena cava and complete 
atrioventricular septal defect were significantly more prev-
alent in patients who underwent SVP. This suggests that het-
erotaxy may remain an important factor in choosing SVP 
over BVR, potentially because of associated systemic and 
pulmonary venous anomalies complicating intracardiac 
repair in BVR. 34,35 These anatomic considerations should 
remain key factors in surgical decision making for DORV 
patients, particularly in borderline cases where either 
BVR or SVP could be performed.
Additionall y, our findings support a patient-speci fic, 
anatomy-dri ven approach to DORV surgical decision 
making. Given its superior survival and lower transplanta -
tion rates compared to SVP, BVR should be prioritized  
whenever technically  feasible. Patients with ncVSD and 
subpulmonic  VSD DORV should not be excluded auto-
matically;  rather, when there is clinical uncertainty  
regarding feasibility  of BVR, we recommend consultation  
with a high-volume  center experienced  in complex DORV 
repair. Furthermor e, patients selected for SVP require 
close surveillance  for late complications,  particularly  
collateral  formation,  valve dysfunction,  and progressive 
heart failure.
Study Limitations
Several limitations are present in our study. As a single-
center retrospective study, our findings might not be gener-
alizable to other centers, which may use different surgical 
approaches for each DORV variant. Additionally, selection 
bias may have influenced the initial decision making pro-
cess regarding whether patients underwent BVR or SVP. 
Our cohort included some patients who were diagnosed 
and underwent surgical procedures at other institutions 
before presenting to our center, which complicated compar-
isons, particularly regarding operative data and follow-up 
care prior to presentation at our institution.
Furthermor e, our study spans 4 decades of managing 
patients with DORV, introducin g a potential era effect, 
as advances in surgical techniques,  perioperati ve manage-
ment, and follow-up care may have influenced outcomes. 
Improvements in BVR and SVP techniques  and postoper-
ative monitoring  may have led to better survival and 
reduced morbidity in recent years compared to earlier 
cohorts.

Future studies should aim to incorporate new technolo-
gies, such as advanced preoperative imaging and computa-
tional modeling, into the risk stratification and surgical 
decision making process for DORV patients. Advances in 
3-dimensional modeling and printing techniques have shown 
potential in guiding intracardiac rerouting strategies, allow-
ing for patient-specific surgical planning to optimize BVR 
feasibility and outcomes. 36 Incorporating these technologies, 
as well as newer surgical techniques, into multicenter collab-
orations may further refine patient selection and improve 
long-term surgical outcomes for DORV patients.
CONCLUSIONS
Our study demonstrates that BVR is associated with su-
perior long-term survival and reduced transplantation rates 
compared to SVP in DORV patients. Although BVR may be 
technically challenging for ncVSD and subpulmonic VSD, 
our findings suggest that BVR can be successful in these pa-
tients with proper surgical planning, reinforcing its role as 
the preferred approach whenever feasible. Our BVR group 
had reinterventions primarily associated with outflow tract 
and pulmonary valve procedures, whereas our SVP patients 
primarily had reinterventions associated with SVP compli-
cations. Additionally, such factors as heterotaxy and sys-
temic/pulmonary venous anomalies also may influence 
surgical decision making by favoring SVP in certain cases. 
Our study provides important data regarding the long-term 
outcomes of patients who underwent BVR or SVP repairs 
for DORV, highlighting ideas for future investigations 
regarding health-related quality of life. Multicenter valida-
tion is needed to further refine BVR eligibility criteria for 
DORV patients.
