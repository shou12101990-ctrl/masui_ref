cardium (FAP) in patients with pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum (PA-IVS).

METHODS: Nine patients with PA-IVS underwent TV leaflet augmentation (TVLA) using FAP between January 2017 and April 2024. 
Native TV failure was defined as significant (≥moderate) tricuspid regurgitation and/or stenosis (mean pressure gradient >5 mmHg). 
Clinical outcomes, including both TV and right ventricle (RV) size and functions, were evaluated using echocardiography and magnetic 
resonance imaging.
RESULTS: The median age and body weight at the time of surgery were 5.4 years and 21.2 kg, respectively. No operative mortality oc -
curred. During the follow-up period, no late mortalities, reoperations or significant TV leaflet-related complications were observed; na -
tive TV failure was not observed in any patients, except one with significant tricuspid regurgitation. Biventricular repair was performed in 
all patients except one who had undergone preoperative one-and-a-half ventricle repair. After TVLA using FAP, the median TV annulus 
z-score remained within the normal ranges (preoperative −0.42 [−0.62 to 0.57] versus postoperative −0.51 [−0.95 to −0.13], mean differ -
ence: 0.231, 95% confidence interval: −0.338 to 0.800, P ¼ 0.377) and tricuspid regurgitation improved significantly (mean difference: 
2.111, 95% confidence interval: 1.510–2.712, P ¼ 0.040). Postoperative magnetic resonance imaging showed that RV function, and cardiac 
output were preserved within acceptable ranges.
CONCLUSIONS: TVLA using FAP could be a considerable and viable approach to optimize RV growth while preserving RV and TV func -
tions in the long-term in young patients with PA-IVS.

INTRODUCTION
Pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum (PA-IVS) encom -
passes a broad spectrum of congenital heart diseases, characterized 
by varying degrees of right ventricular (RV) and tricuspid valve (TV) 
hypoplasia, along with coronary artery anomalies. The manage -
ment of PA-IVS is challenging owing to its inherent heterogeneity, 
resulting in diverse approaches to definitive repair and varying sur -
gical outcomes [ 1 , 2 ]. In patients with PA-IVS, the TV is commonly 
hypoplastic and dysplastic, frequently presenting with tricuspid ste -
nosis (TS) and/or regurgitation (TR) [ 2 , 3 ]. Given the complex TV 
and RV morphology in PA-IVS, surgical treatment may be required 
to achieve sustainable biventricular repair (BVR) with favour -
able outcomes.
In our experience, we have often encountered issues related to 
TV function, such as TR and TS, which arise from the complex TV 
dysmorphology. Managing TV function may be critical in promot -
ing healthy RV growth, not merely RV enlargement due to TR. 
Moreover, TS may impede effective RV growth. Therefore, address -
ing TV dysfunction while preserving RV function is critical. 
However, despite the prevalence of TV abnormalities, few studies 
have addressed TV function and the outcomes of TV repair in 
patients with PA-IVS. Furthermore, the complexity of the TV ren -
ders managing TV dysfunction challenging and complicates the se -
lection of appropriate surgical techniques in these patients.
At our institution, the RV overhaul (RVOh) procedure has 
been performed to maximize the RV decompression and estab -
lish adequate antegrade pulmonary blood flow through the RV, 
ultimately promoting RV growth in patients with PA-IVS [ 4 , 5 ]. 
As part of this approach, we have employed various surgical 
techniques to manage TV dysfunction, and since 2017, TV leaflet 
augmentation (TVLA) using fresh autologous pericardium (FAP) 
was introduced for patients with appropriate indications. 
Consequently, this technique is expected to contribute to ensur -
ing adequate forward flow through the RV by improving TV 
function, thereby promoting RV growth while preserving RV 
function. In addition, FAP has been reported as a feasible and 
useful option for valve repair [ 6–12 ].
This study aimed to comprehensively evaluate the surgical 
outcomes of TVLA using FAP, focusing on its feasibility and dura -
bility in patients with PA-IVS. The study sought to determine 
whether TVLA using FAP could maintain somatic growth in 
right-sided cardiac structures. This was assessed through quanti -
tative measurements of RV size and function using magnetic res -
onance imaging (MRI) during follow-up.
MATERIAL AND METHODS
Ethics statement
The study was reviewed and approved as a minimal-risk retrospec -
tive study that did not require individual consent based on the in -
stitutional guidelines for waiving consent by the Seoul National 
University Hospital Institutional Review Board (approval number: 
H-2409-087-1570; approval date: 2 October 2024).
Patients
Overall, 14 TV repairs were performed in 13 patients with PA- 
IVS at our institution between January 2017 and April 2024. Of 
these, five TV repairs without leaflet augmentation, performed in 
four patients, were excluded. None of the patients in the study 
underwent TVLA using other materials, such as glutaraldehyde- 
treated autologous pericardium or bovine pericardium. Finally, 
nine consecutive patients who underwent TVLA using FAP were 
included in the study. Of these, six patients underwent RVOh

Surgical indications and technique
RVOh is an integrative surgical approach aimed at maximizing 
RV decompression, improving RV compliance and promoting 
RV growth in patients with PA-IVS. Our RVOh procedure 
involves RV sinus myectomy, including resection of hypertro -
phied RV muscles and endocardial fibrotic tissue, combined 
with valvuloplasty of the pulmonary valve (PV) and TV as clini -
cally indicated.
Surgical indications for TVLA using FAP were persistent TV 
steno-insufficiency with significant (≥moderate) TR during medi -
cal follow-up (3–6-month period) after the initial identification 
of significant TR, accompanied by (i) a large central coaptation 
gap, (ii) no TV annular dilatation (z-score > 2.0), (iii) severe re -
strictive motion of TV leaflets due to hypoplastic and short chor -
dae and/or chordal tethering and (iv) insufficient TV leaflet 
tissue. Based on these indications, and considering the hypoplas -
tic and dysplastic nature of the TV with a severely restrictive 
valve apparatus, TVLA using FAP was performed at the surgeon’s 
discretion. An appropriately oversized patch may compensate 
for the lack of tissue and improve leaflet pliability. Additionally, 
given the larger surface area of the TV anterior leaflet and its 
critical role in maintaining TV function, we opted for TV anterior 
leaflet augmentation to maximize its potential effectiveness for 
improving TV competence with optimal geometry. To this end, 
TV anterior leaflet augmentation can achieve this goal by en -
hancing leaflet mobility and creating a larger coaptation surface.
A segment of autologous pericardium was harvested from the an -
terior aspect of the heart, dissected free of fatty and connective tis -
sue and tailored to the appropriate size and shape corresponding to 
the required size of the TV. In redo cases, particular care was taken 
to identify well-preserved and intact pericardium and to avoid any 
damage during preparation. For the anterior leaflet of TV, an incision 
was made �2 mm from the annulus and extended to both commis -
sures, avoiding injury to the subvalvular apparatus. The patch was 
implanted using continuous 6–0 or 7–0 Prolene sutures. The patch 
was trimmed to a size larger than the estimated dimensions required 
for the appropriate coaptation of the TV (Fig. 2). In addition to TVLA 
using FAP, other TV repairs, such as papillary muscle splitting, artifi -
cial chordae implantation, secondary chordae resection and minimal 
annuloplasty, were performed concomitantly to manage TV func -
tion. After TV repair, including augmentation using FAP, cold saline 
was directly injected into the RV to assess for TR. Based on the saline 
injection test and postoperative transoesophageal echocardiography, 
if needed, additional TV repair was performed to reduce the degree 
of TR while preventing TS. Consequently, the geometry and function 
of the TV were maintained and restored as much as possible during 
the TV repair.

Magnetic resonance imaging assessment
MRI scans were conducted using a 1.5-T unit (Avanto; Siemens, 
Erlangen, Germany) during follow-up. The CMR protocol included 
thoracic survey images captured from axial, coronal and sagittal 
angles, cine sequences utilizing steady-state free precession in two- 
and four-chamber views, right ventricular outflow tract view and 
short-axis stacks spanning from the base to the apex. The cine se -
quence parameters were as follows: 6-mm slice thickness; 300 × 
300 mm field of view, 240 × 216 matrix; 45.63 ms temporal resolu -
tion. Also, phase-contrast velocity encoding cine was performed or -
thogonal to the main pulmonary artery plane to evaluate pulmonary 
regurgitation fraction. The analysis was performed using the Argus 
post-processing tool (Siemens Medical Solutions). RV endocardial 
contours were obtained from the short-axis view in a semi- 
automated manner, excluding papillary muscles and trabeculae from 
the cavity. RV systolic and diastolic volumes were determined using 
Simpson’s rule.
Follow-up and evaluation of clinical outcomes
All patients underwent regular postoperative follow-up. Data on 
survival status and presence of cardiovascular events were collected 
from electronic medical records. Information on reoperation and 
occurrence of major adverse events was collected. The median 
clinical and echocardiographic durations were 4.3 (3.5–4.7) years 
and 3.4 (3.2–4.5) years, respectively. Operative mortality was de -
fined as death within 30 days postoperatively or during the same 
hospitalization. Late mortality was defined as death following dis -
charge. Native TV failure (NTVF) was defined as significant 
(≥moderate) TR and/or TS (mean pressure gradient >5 mmHg).
Statistical analysis
Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard devia -
tion and median (interquartile range). Categorical variables are 
presented as frequencies (%). Paired T-test was performed to 
compare the preoperative and postoperative values. Event-free 
rates were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method.
Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 25.0 
IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All P-values were two-tailed. 
Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

DISCUSSION
The RVOh is a comprehensive strategy for PA-IVS that includes 
not only RV sinus myectomy but also TV and PV repairs [ 4 , 5 , 16 , 
17 ]. This approach is critical in promoting RV growth by maxi -
mizing its decompression and ensuring adequate forward flow 
through the RV [ 4 , 5 , 16 , 17 ]. Within this framework, the goal of 
TV repair in the RVOh procedure is to restore forward flow 
through the RV, while minimizing pathological RV dilatation due 
to TR.
We previously introduced our strategy for PA-IVS without RV- 
dependent coronary circulation, including RVOh procedure, 
with favourable outcomes [ 4 , 5 ]. Since 2017, as part of this com -
prehensive overhaul procedure, TVLA using FAP has been 
employed to improve TV function and restore optimal valve ge -
ometry. The leaflet augmentation helps enhance leaflet mobility 
and create a larger coaptation surface, thus contributing to bet -
ter TV competence. Alongside PV repair, the inclusion of TV re -
pair in the RVOh strategy further supports improved antegrade 
pulmonary blood flow. Our experience showed that this inte -
grated approach, including TV repair, such as TVLA using FAP, 
led to improved outcomes for optimal RV growth, as well as 
both TV and PV function, supporting sustained BVR while pre -
serving RV function [ 4 , 5 ].
The present study revealed two main findings. First, the me -
dian TV annulus z-score was maintained after TVLA using FAP, 
with growth trending towards the normal ranges rather than 
remaining disproportionately small. Second, postoperative MRI 
showed preserved RV ejection fraction, stroke volume index and 
cardiac output index despite elevated RV end-diastolic and end- 
systolic indices.
Role of tricuspid valve function management 
within the right ventricle overhaul strategy
The ultimate treatment goal for PA-IVS is to promote RV growth 
to achieve BVR, and various treatment strategies for BVR have 
been introduced [ 1 , 2 , 16 , 17 ]. Previous studies have focused on 
the growth of the RV and TV, since these are key factors for suc -
cessful BVR in patients with PA-IVS and borderline RV hypopla -
sia [ 1 , 2 , 16 , 17 ]. However, unlike the primary focus on the 
growth of the RV and TV, the functional aspect of the TV has 
been relatively overlooked in these patients, despite its poten -
tially critical impact on long-term outcomes. RV enlargement 
resulting from TR may signify pathological RV remodelling due 
to volume overload. We believe that this pathological RV en -
largement, which is unlikely to preserve long-term RV function, 
would be different from ideal and true RV growth. Therefore, we 
believe that preserving TV function is essential for promoting 
healthy RV growth and maintaining its function while preventing 
pathological RV enlargement. Additionally, maintaining and op -
timizing TV function is not only critical for preserving RV func -
tion but also plays a crucial role in ensuring and increasing 
antegrade pulmonary blood flow through the RV. This forward 
flow is essential for promoting effective RV growth because in -
sufficient TV function could reduce forward flow, impeding RV 
growth. Without proper management of TV function, including 
TR and TS, persistently uncontrolled TV dysfunction may result 
in insufficient RV growth and lead to irreversible RV dysfunction 
over time, making RV growth less meaningful and potentially af -
fecting long-term outcomes. Although RV volume overload fol -
lowing TR can increase RV size, managing TV function may be a 
critical component in optimizing RV growth and performance 
by ensuring adequate forward flow through the RV in patients 
with PA-IVS. Therefore, we addressed TV dysfunction to

Technical challenges and solutions
Previous studies have raised significant concerns about the use of 
FAP in valve repair compared with other materials, citing issues 
such as progressive contracture and loss of pliability, which might 
contribute to its relatively negative outcomes [ 18 , 21 , 22 ]. The ma -
jor concerns included shrinkage, size mismatch, leaflet distortion or 
retraction and leaflet dehiscence in the durability of FAP [ 10 , 18 ]. 
However, several recent studies have highlighted favourable out -
comes of valve repair using FAP [ 8–12 ].
Valve leaflet reconstruction with FAP to prevent aforemen -
tioned problems may be technically challenging [ 10 , 18 ]. In the 
present study, these issues on FAP were minimized by precisely 
tailoring the FAP to fit the size and shape of the TV leaflets, 
slightly oversizing the patch for optimal coaptation. Our findings 
align with those of previous studies showing that FAP is effective 
in maintaining the structural integrity of the valve over time [ 8– 
10 , 12 ]. Throughout the follow-up period, no signs of valve leaf -
let retraction, distortion or dehiscence were observed. However, 
an equivocal increase in echogenicity, possibly suggestive of 
minimal calcification on the TV, was observed in two patients. 
Nonetheless, these findings were not significant and did not ap -
pear to be related to any appreciable TV dysfunction.
Clinical outcomes and potential benefits by 
tricuspid valve leaflet augmentation using fresh 
autologous pericardium
The primary goal of our study was to evaluate the effects of 
TVLA using FAP on both TV and RV growth and function. 
Postoperative MRI assessments showed that RV function was 
largely maintained within normal ranges across the patient co -
hort [ 23 , 24 ]. Furthermore, RV end-diastolic and end-systolic vol -
ume indices were elevated in most patients, likely reflecting pre- 
existing RV volume overload due to preoperative TR and pulmo -
nary regurgitation [ 23 , 24 ]. However, the postoperative RV stroke 
volume index and cardiac output index remained stable, sug -
gesting that RV growth was proportionate to the patient’s overall 
growth, while ensuring adequate RV function [ 23 , 24 ]. These 
findings indicate that while some degree of pathological RV en -
largement might remain in some patients, effectively and timely 
addressing TV function issues might not only minimize further 
pathological RV enlargement but also maintain adequate ante -
grade pulmonary blood flow. If left untreated, unresolved TV 
function issues may lead to progressive RV dilatation and dys -
function due to chronic volume overload. Failing to maintain 
adequate antegrade pulmonary blood flow, which is essential 
for sustaining RV growth and function, may ultimately impair RV 
growth. Thus, our findings support that TVLA using FAP could 
achieve the key goal of our treatment strategy by improving 
long-term outcomes in young patients with PA-IVS.
Hence, our findings highlight the importance of not only focus -
ing on RV and TV growth but also prioritizing functional TV resto -
ration to ensure long-term RV performance. Unresolved TV issues 
can lead to progressive RV dysfunction. This emphasizes the need 
for comprehensive TV management in patients with PA-IVS. 
Additionally, our results suggest that TVLA using FAP can address 
TV issues resulting from inherent TV dysmorphology while also op -
timizing RV growth and function. Therefore, TVLA using FAP may 
offer significant long-term benefits for paediatric patients with PA- 
IVS. Although BVR can be achieved without addressing TV issues in 
the early stages, the risk of long-term adverse events related to po -
tential RV dysfunction should be considered. By mitigating TV dys -
function throughout follow-up, this approach may optimize RV 
growth and minimize the likelihood of its potential failure. Given 
these considerations, TVLA using FAP can serve as a reasonable 
and valid interim step to preserve the native TV, considering the 
characteristics of paediatric patients, potentially improving overall 
long-term clinical outcomes.
Limitations
This study has some limitations. First, this was a non- 
randomized, retrospective single-centre study, which may intro -
duce inconsistencies and limit the control of confounding varia -
bles. Second, the small sample size, associated with the rare 
incidence of the disease, may increase variability and reduce the 
precision and robustness of the results. Additionally, although 
no significant structural complications were observed during the 
follow-up period, the reproducibility of the results should be 
confirmed. Moreover, given the disparities in the follow-up du -
ration, long-term studies with larger cohorts and extended 
follow-up are warranted, particularly regarding TV and RV 
growth and functions. Third, the heterogeneity of the study pop -
ulation complicated the control of baseline characteristics and 
perioperative data due to varying clinical statuses. Fourth, most 
participants did not undergo preoperative MRI, and the timing 
of postoperative MRI during the follow-up was inconsistent. 
Finally, this study did not include a comparative analysis with 
patients who did not undergo TV repair, underwent TV repair 
without leaflet augmentation or underwent augmentation with 
other materials, which limits the ability to draw definitive con -
clusions regarding the impact of TV repair or leaflet augmenta -
tion on RV growth. Consequently, it remains challenging to 
conclusively determine whether FAP is superior to other valve 
repair options or whether performing TV repair or leaflet aug -
mentation is beneficial compared to leaving the TV unaddressed. 
Furthermore, TV repair was not intended as an isolated interven -
tion merely to increase the RV size but rather to prevent patho -
logical RV dilatation following TV dysfunction, preserve RV 
function and support healthy RV growth as part of the compre -
hensive RV optimization strategy, particularly RVOh, in the long 
term. Therefore, generalization of the results should be 
approached with caution, and long-term studies with larger 
sample sizes are warranted to validate the results.

CONCLUSION
Early and mid-term outcomes of TVLA using FAP were acceptable 
in young patients with PA-IVS. With appropriate indications, TVLA 
using FAP could be a considerable and viable approach to opti -
mize RV growth while preserving RV and TV functions.
